## 1. Premise

A new tamer arrives in Thistledown on the morning after the sky flickered. The kindly mentor hands over a first friend, a cocky boy loses a battle to them on the village green, and a glint by the newcomer's boot goes into the boy's pocket. From there the player walks the ring of eleven lands round the Crown, the mountain at the world's centre, earning a badge in each town.

The ring is coming apart. Ten years ago the sky tore over Thistledown. A vast cloud-ram has held the tear small ever since, from its seat on the Crown, and it is getting tired. What leaks through drives the giants wild and turns creatures to gem. Something waits on the far side of the tear and pulls at the world's seams: the Unraveller.

**In one line:** a cosy creature journey in which every friend you make is patched together from someone else's, and the enemy is the thing that wants to unpick it all.

## 2. Theme: handed down, patched up

The research notes that breeding and trading are rarely used by these stories, and that a lineage or family theme fits the "family-scale stakes" pattern (research sections 2.5 and 6). We use it because it already runs through the game's systems.

- **Every creature is a patchwork.** Breeding mixes parts from two parents, so every bred creature is handed down. The world's myth explains why: the Firstling stitched the first creatures from its own glass panes, so every part in the game is one of its patches (scaffold F).
- **The story's gifts are hand-me-downs.** The mentor's first friend and his egg (chapter 1), each town's token, the Frayling (chapter 9), and the rival's own first creature (chapter 10, [Locked] as a gift). Trading, when it ships, is the same act between players.
- **The lasso is a thread used kindly.** The player ties, the Unraveller unpicks, and the Firstling stitches. One motif covers capture, the villain and the legendary.

Three answers to "what do you do with what you were handed?" make up the cast:

| Who | Their answer | Where it leads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The mentor | Make it perfect. No patches. | He bred with a fallen star and tore the sky |
| The Unraveller (through the rival) | Unpick it. Hold nothing, and nothing can be taken | The rival turns on everyone who gave him anything |
| Sorrel, then the player | Mend it, and let the mend show | Gold seams: the Frayling mended, the bridge mended, the sky stitched shut |

The theme is said once, by a Cinderwick glassblower in chapter 7, and Sorrel takes it as her own: "Fill it with gold, so the mend shows." It is never said again. The rest is shown.

## 3. Cast

Speaker labels in the data stay as roles until Ayrton names people ("Mentor", "Rival", "Ringwalker", "Unraveller"), as chapters 1 to 4 do. Sorrel is new, so she carries a placeholder name.

### The player

- **Role:** a new tamer, just arrived in Thistledown ([Locked]: "a new tamer from Greenwood"). Everyday title "Wrangler" (the brief's working title, and it suits the lasso).
- **The job (pattern 6):** to walk the ring. Long before the player, tamers called Ringwalkers walked the whole ring round the Crown, carried news between towns, and kept the roads and the giants calm. Nobody has done it for years. The towns give the player work (quests) because that is what a walker is for. At the end the player is named Ringwalker, the old title.
- **Voice:** the player speaks rarely, as "You", and mostly to say what they see when no character can (a creature cannot talk). Wry, short, never a speech.

### The mentor

- **On the surface:** a dotty old breeder with a cottage on the green, a kettle always on and three creatures on his porch. "Seemingly naive" ([Locked]).
- **Underneath:** the finest breeder Thistledown ever had. Ten years ago he tried to breed a perfect bloodline using a fallen star. The "star" was a pane of the Firstling's glass, and using it tore the sky over Thistledown ([Locked]: he caused the tear by accident; [Default]: the breeding experiment and the fragment). The egg hatched one small, half-finished creature, the Frayling. He hid it at Starfall Crater and cut the bridge so no one would find it. His oldest friend, the Ringwalker, went to mend the sky and never came back. He told her grandchildren she was "travelling". He has lied ever since ([Locked]: he keeps lying to protect the player).
- **Want:** that nobody else he loves walks into the tear. **Need:** to tell the truth and be forgiven. **Wound:** he thinks he killed his best friend.
- **His tell:** he says "Truly" only when he is lying. It is already in chapters 2, 3 and 4. Sorrel points it out in chapter 5, so players start to watch for it. In chapter 9 he says "No 'truly' this time."
- **Arc:** lies (1 to 8) → found out by the Ringwalker's last page (9) → confesses → blocks the Starwell himself and loses (9) → lets the player go → forgiven, and home with the Ringwalker (11).
- **Teaches:** the starter and the lasso (1), breeding (1, the egg), and, as the chapter 9 gate, that a team of friends can beat a team bred for perfect.
- **Patterns:** 7 (a reveal about the mentor), 5 (family-scale stakes).

### The rival

- **Who:** a sharp-eyed boy in a green jacket with a loud, quick grin. The Ringwalker's grandson. Everyone in Thistledown has told him all his life that he will never be her.
- **Want:** to be first at something. **Need:** to stop measuring himself by who left him. **Wound:** his gran walked away when he was six; he has always been second to a ghost.
- **His first creature** hatched from the egg his gran left him. He picked it for its looks, it is his pride, and it loses to the player's starter on the green (the data already gives him the starter that loses). After Greenwood he sends it home: "It only knew how to lose." Sorrel brings it to him in chapter 5; it saves him in chapter 8; he gives it to the player in chapter 10. That makes the [Locked] gift cost him the most it can, and it is why the creature is "the one he has kept since Greenwood" in spirit if not in his pocket (see `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`, question 4).
- **The thread:** the glint he pockets in chapter 1 is a shard with a black thread knotted through it: Blackstitch, a splinter of the Unraveller (scaffold F). It hums (2), it asks him things (3), it promises the Mire's giant will kneel (4). After the giant throws him, it tells him to swallow it, and he does, between chapters 4 and 5. Nobody sees this. From chapter 5 the shards are gone ("Don't need them now"), his eyes catch red for a blink, and his creatures wear dark stitches. The player learns what he swallowed only after the Unraveller is destroyed ([Locked], arc item 8).
- **Arc** ([Locked] items 1, 2, 5, 6, 8 and brief section 14): loses (1) → dark tactics: shoves past a warden, steals a punt, tries to rope a giant (2 to 4) → swallows the thread; cold, cruel to his sister, giants taken by force (5 to 7) → at the tear, his first creature shields him and he refuses the voice: friend again (8) → reads his gran's last page and breaks the Starwell seal to fetch her (9) → the last rival battle, a warning and the gift (10) → confesses the thread, then leaves to walk the ring alone with it still asleep in him (11): the stage 3 threat.
- **His team tells the story** (brief [Default]): Greenwood creatures (1) → gem-touched, tint 0.25 to 0.85 (2 to 4) → a stitched giant in the team, tint 0.9 to 1 (5, 7) → his own creatures, no stitches, tint about 0.5 (10).
- **Patterns:** 3 (a rival with an arc), 5, 8 (his gift and his leaving are the ending's cost).

### Sorrel, the companion

- **Who:** the rival's little sister, about twelve. Blunt, practical, very funny without trying. She mends things: cracked cups, cracked clay, and later cracked creatures.
- **Arrives:** chapter 5, at the Rimewick healer's door, carrying her brother's first creature. She has walked up the Rimewater Gorge alone to fetch him home.
- **Want:** to bring her brother home. **Need:** to learn that you can't fix a person, only stand by them. **Wound:** at home nobody says Gran's name.
- **Her own thread (pattern 4):** she learns gold-seam mending from a Cinderwick glassblower (7), mends the Frayling with it (9) and helps the Frayling mend the Last Bridge (10). She finds her gran (11) and, at the very end, lets her brother walk away: "You'd better write. With real letters. No 'truly'."
- **What she gives the story:** the mentor's tell ("he only says truly when he's fibbing"), the reveal that the journal pages are her gran's handwriting (5), the jokes in the dark chapters, and the reaction shots a silent player cannot give.
- **Teaches by asking:** cross-biome breeding (5), "mended" (7, 9).
- **Never:** a tutorial voice. She never tells the player which button to press.

### The Ringwalker (the old tamer)

- **Who:** the rival's and Sorrel's gran, the last tamer to walk the whole ring, and the mentor's oldest friend. She writes the journal pages players find from Greenwood on (plan 14: "one unnamed old tamer"). Her name is [Open].
- **What happened:** after the sky tore, she walked the ring looking for a way to mend it. At the Heartstone she learned of the Firstling. Her last page, at the Mirror Mere cottage, looks down on Thistledown's roofs: she turned away from home and went through the Starwell into the crater. Lanternmoor lit the Lantern Spire's lamp for her that night, blinking the walkers' code for "come home", and it has blinked every night since. Players can see it from Greenwood on day one.
- **Now:** alive, at Old Hearth in the Hollow, keeping a fire. Thread burns, so her fire has kept the Unraveller's fingers off the sleeping Firstling for eight years. Her old hound, the giant Hearthwarden, has gone wild with the tear's leak.
- **Voice:** dry, warm, salty. "Well. You took your time."
- **Patterns:** 5, 9 (place carries story: the pages, the lamp, the cottage), 7.

### The Unraveller, the villain

- **What:** the entity behind the tear ([Locked]: the final boss, a character, and purely evil). A towering mass of black yarn with a seam-ripper head and one crimson bead for an eye (scaffold F). Where the Firstling stitches, it unpicks.
- **Its argument (pattern 1):** "Every stitch in you was put there by someone else. Hold nothing, and nothing can be taken from you." It is a lure, not a belief: the Unraveller wants nothing but to undo. It offers each person what they lack: perfection to the mentor (through the star), freedom from being second to the rival, an end to loss to the player. It never raises its voice and never threatens; it speaks like someone doing you a favour.
- **Present from chapter 1 (pattern 2):** the glint by the player's boot (1), the hum (2), the question (3), the promise (4), the argument in the rival's mouth (5), its picture in the Heartstone (6), the stitched stag (7), its own voice from the tear (8), the cause of the mentor's secret (9), a whisper to the player at the Rift (10), and the 6v6 finale (11).
- **Plan:** it cannot reach through the tear itself, so it works through hands. It used the mentor's ambition to open the seam, it uses the rival to bind the giants (the oldest knots in the world's patchwork) and to cut Nimbram's hold, and in the end it wants the sleeping Firstling's last thread.
- **End:** the player destroys it ([Locked], arc item 7). Its splinter, Blackstitch, goes quiet in the rival and does not die ([Locked], stage 3: "the dormant evil in the rival reawakens").

### Blackstitch

A fist-sized living knot of black thread with one crimson bead eye (scaffold F), hidden in the glint. It has no lines of its own: everything it says reaches the player through the rival ("It asked me why I keep things I never chose"). Not a creature at launch. The gifted first creature keeps one black stitch that flickers in the last beat: the sequel seed ([Default]).

### The Firstling, the legendary

- **What:** the first creature, a long stained-glass dragon with a golden hoop (Ayrton's model; scaffold F). It sleeps under the Hollow holding the world's seams shut. One pane is missing from its brow: the "star" that fell at Starfall Crater.
- **As a character (pattern 10):** it never speaks; it acts. It is seen first as a picture in the Heartstone (6) and on a living tapestry (11). When it wakes it laughs like wind chimes and sniffs every patch on the player's team, delighted, like meeting great-grandchildren: it wants to see what its creatures became, and that is why it goes with the player. It stitches the sky shut with a thread from its own mane and hands the player the end: the cosmic wrap ([Locked], arc item 9).
- **Its choice:** it does not take its chip back. The Frayling gives it.

### The Frayling

The mentor's mistake (scaffold F): small, frightened, two panes missing with stars showing through, and the Firstling's chip in its brow. Afraid of the open sky. Sorrel mends it with gold seams (9), and the mentor asks the player to take it and "show it everything I was afraid of". It mends the Last Bridge (10) and, unasked, gives its chip back to the Firstling (11), which lets the sky close. Afterwards its brow is just a gold seam, and it looks lighter. It never speaks.

### Nimbram

The vast cloud-ram whose fleece is the halo on the Crown (scaffold F). It caught the tear the night it opened, dragged it from Thistledown to the Crown and has held it small ever since. Its fleece greys a shade each chapter (plan 13). It is first seen in chapter 8 from Cloud Isle: the halo is not cloud but fleece, and it is breathing hard. It never speaks or fights in the story. After the finale its fleece is white again.

## 6. Tone rules

1. **Funny, then felt.** Most beats get a joke or a warm line; the ones that matter get the joke first and the feeling after, never the other way round (pattern 11).
2. **Teen-safe, not childish.** Real stakes and real mistakes; no baby talk, no "love and friendship fixes everything" without a cost (research failures, S6). Nothing gory or cruel: the scariest image is a toy coming apart at the seams.
3. **Show, don't tell.** The tint on the rival's team, the halo's shade, the lamp, the stitches, the kettle. A line explains only what the screen can't show.
4. **Teach once, then go quiet.** Each town's idea is taught by one local in one beat; later lines may remind, never re-teach (research section 5).
5. **The player does it.** Every big moment is a battle the player wins, a place they walk into or a person they talk to: the plot happens through the player (research failure: "in front of the player").
6. **The villain never shouts.** No exclamation marks for the Unraveller, ever.
7. **The mentor says "Truly" only when he lies.** Never in a true line, except the Ringwalker's joke in chapter 11.
8. **No in-jokes about other games, no series lore.** A newcomer must follow every line (research failure: Z-A).
9. **Warm names, plain words.** Places and people sound like an old storybook village; no jargon, no "stat buff".

## 7. Voice sheets

Each has a rule set and sample lines (all under 90 characters).

### The mentor
- No contractions ("I will", "do not"), except his catchphrase, "Kettle's on" (chapter 1's first line). Short, mild sentences. Tea, kettles, mending. Deflects with a small joke.
- Lies end with "Truly." Truth comes plain and a little slow.
- "Kettle's on. Sit. No, not on the cat."
- "Only a pebble. Truly."
- "I cannot lose anyone else. Please."
- "No 'truly' this time. Just true."

### The rival
- Contractions, speed, boasts, snacks (chapter 1's joke). Gets shorter and colder as the tint grows, then awkward and plain when he turns.
- "Mine is way cooler, though. Just so you know."
- "It doesn't hurt to lose anymore. That's the part that scares me."
- "Back to back. Like we were ever on the same side. ...We were, weren't we?"
- "Here. My first. It never quit on me, even when I quit on it."

### Sorrel
- Deadpan, practical, lists things, counts things. Admits fear sideways. Never cries on screen; gets very busy instead.
- "I'm small, I'm useful, and I mend things."
- "Two trulys in one letter. That's a record."
- "I'm not scared. I'm holding your sleeve for your sake."
- "Mended things mend things. I'm writing that down."

### The Ringwalker
- Dry, warm, old-traveller sayings, calls people "love". Says the hard thing kindly.
- "Well. You took your time."
- "Thread burns, see. That's the whole trick."
- "It was his mistake. That is not the same as his fault."

### The Unraveller
- Soft, polite, patient; sewing words (loosen, pull, unpick, thread, hold); calls people "little one". Full stops only.
- "Shh. I am only loosening it."
- "Hold nothing, and nothing can be taken from you."
- "There you all are. The patched, the mended, the handed-down. How tidy."

### The player ("You")
- Rare. Says what is seen, not what is felt; lets the player feel it.
- "Two of its patches are missing. Stars show through."

### Locals
- One trait each, one job each, never longer than two lines unless they teach the town's idea. Each town has a running joke on its sign ("Toadstools are not chairs").

## 8. Line rules (the data's, kept exactly)

- At most three lines a beat, at most 90 characters a line (`WorldTypes.DialogueLine`; `tests/world.test.luau` checks this for real chapters).
- One-tap skippable; skipping never loses a gift (the grant is on the beat, not the line).
- Every beat has a goal the tracker can show; where the derived hint reads badly the draft gives a `hint`.
- Triggers are only Start, Follow, Enter, Sight, Win, Tame and Talk, and each beat needs the flag of the beat before, so the storyline reads straight on.
- A payoff (a reveal, a rival turn, a creature moment) about every 10 to 20 minutes of play (research section 4). Story beats space out as chapters lengthen; quests, finds and the giant fill the gaps (see `ARC.md`, pacing).
