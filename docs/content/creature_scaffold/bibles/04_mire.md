# Design bible 04: The Mire (Toxic), town Sedgeholm, 4v4

Date: 23 November 2026. Status: [Default] design guidance for scaffold agent B and the Meshy prompts. Roster, types, plans and roles are fixed by `00_ROSTER_PLAN.md` section 3 (Fenwhisk, Snaggar, Bogbellow, Mistrel, Skipslick, Keelsnap, Bulbroth, Lilyshade; giant Sloughback). Part rules are `00_BREEDING_STANDARD.md`. Principles only; nothing here copies another franchise's creature.

## 1. Visual identity

**Mood in one line:** a warm, damp bedtime story with something watching from the reeds. Creepy-cute, never gross: toxic means toys (bubbles, drips, spots), not rot. The loved evidence (`loved/02_BY_TYPE_EARLY.md`, section 4) finds poison loved as a mood, and realistic slime in no poll.

**Palette** (two main colours plus one accent per creature, from this set):

| Role | Name | Hex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main | Olive | #6B7A3A |
| Main | Peat brown | #5A4632 |
| Main | Moss green | #8FA24A |
| Main | Bruise violet | #6E4C86 |
| Support | Fog grey-green | #B9C4A8 |
| Support | Muddy cream (bellies) | #D9CFA3 |
| Accent only | Toxic lime | #B8E629 |
| Accent only | Lantern coral (eyes, spots) | #F08A5D |

Rules: lime is never a main colour and covers under 10% of a creature. Bruise violet is the "poisonous" colour and lives on one part only (belly, back spots or tail). The rest sits in the olive-peat-moss band so the biome reads as one family beside Tide Flats' blues and Frostline's whites. Contrast comes from dark peat against pale fog and cream.

**Materials** (matte plasticine clay toy): glossy wet-look clay only on eyes, bubbles and drips; matte lumpy clay for hide; moss as short, chunky rolled strands; nothing see-through (bubbles are opaque pale panels, painted bright, not glowing).

**Shape language:** swollen, drooping, lumpy. Heavy bellies, sagging jowls, hunched shoulders, sleepy half-lids. Each creature gets one drooping curve in its silhouette (a hanging lip, a bowed back, a sagging tail). Round over spiky; teeth are small blocks, never fangs.

**Motifs** (each used by at most two species, so none turns to wallpaper): bubbles, drips, hanging moss, lily pads, reed heads, fog, mud-caked patches, spore dots, half-lidded eyes, a lopsided grin.

**World fit** (`13_WORLD_DESIGN.md`): fog, fireflies, frogs and a distant bell; mangroves then cypress, duckboards, the drowned Bell Tower. Creatures stand against dark green-brown water, so each needs one pale or warm patch that pops at phone size.

## 2. Target mix across the eight species

| Mood | Count | Species | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cool | 2 | Snaggar (lurking gar-jaw), Fenwhisk (smug weasel with a reed-flag tail) | In the global top 40, cool is 19, cute 13, majestic 8: cool wins most often. The Mire's predator animals give real silhouettes |
| Cute | 2 | Skipslick (warning-colour frog), Bogbellow (jolly boar) | Round, chubby, one big joke. The chubby smiling common wins fans; a menacing one does not |
| Majestic | 1 | Mistrel (moss-draped tapir in its own fog) | A calm, graceful, large face for the biome, so the set is not all grimace |
| Creepy-cute | 3 | Bulbroth, Lilyshade, Keelsnap | Fans love vulnerability under a spooky surface, and ghosts are over-represented in the polls. Three of eight suits the one biome whose theme is the mood. Keelsnap is the gentle end, Lilyshade the eerie end |

Why not more cool: too many predators bury the personality hooks and turn a swamp into menace. Why not more cute: the Japan polls skew round and sweet, but Roblox favours strong, cool silhouettes and trading, so playtests decide; re-check the mix after the first sessions. The loved evidence puts canids, felids and dragons at about half the favourites and the Mire has none, so the newt giant carries the dragon-adjacent appeal and Fenwhisk the fox-weasel appeal.

## 3. Archetypes from the loved evidence that fit

1. **One signature feature grown into the body**: Fenwhisk's reed tail, Bulbroth's bubbling belly, Mistrel's moss drape, Keelsnap's mud shell. Each owned by one part (standard, section 2.2).
2. **Familiar animal plus a twist**: weasel, boar, tapir, frog, turtle, gar. Players know the animal; the twist is the hook.
3. **Mood, not menace**: a smug, gloomy or grinning face on a chubby body. One readable mood per creature, built into the face.
4. **A hidden or sad story hook**: the drowned Bell Tower gives lore. Lilyshade keeps a pond over a sunk village; Bulbroth cooks for guests who never come. Journal facts carry it.
5. **Two or three colours plus one accent, dark body with one bright patch**: peat or violet with a lime or coral patch.
6. **Warning colours as friendliness**: Skipslick looks dangerous and is a show-off, a toy-like contradiction.
7. **The loved chubby common**: Bogbellow and Skipslick pass "one flaw, one big friendly trait, one silhouette, drawable in ten seconds".

## 4. Rare-look (shiny-style) colour rule

Gem tiers are effects only (sparkle, rim, sheen), so the rare look is a texture variant on the one part that owns the signature feature, and it travels with that part in breeding.

- **Swap the accent and the one violet part; never touch the olive-peat base.** Lime becomes Lantern coral (#F08A5D) and bruise violet becomes deep teal (#2F7F78). A rare Mire creature still reads as Mire, but its accent flips from poison-green to will-o-the-wisp warm.
- **Add one extra detail** on the owner part: Bulbroth gets a second row of bubbles, Mistrel's moss gains tiny coral blossoms, Fenwhisk's reed head turns coral. Rarity reads at a distance through colour and one added shape, not size.
- Painted bright, not glowing; nothing transparent.

## 5. The giant: Sloughback (Toxic, Quad)

**Concept:** the ancient sleeping newt of the Sunken Grove, a hill of a salamander. Peat-brown back grown with real moss, small trees and a lily-pad garden; olive belly; one bruise-violet stripe from head to tail; a patient, half-open eye. It is the swamp itself waking up: calm and enormous, not a monster.

**Fight tell:** the moss and lily pads on its back lie flat one turn before its big Toxic move, and the lime bubble vents on its back open the turn the poison goes out. In its second phase the eyelid lifts fully to show coral eyes and the fog thickens. A player who reads "pads flat means a big hit is coming" can switch to a Sand wall (Moatkeep) or brace. The tell is a visible object state, never a roll. Keep trees and pads on the torso, above the leg tops (standard, section 2.1 rule 3).

## 6. Mascot candidate: Bulbroth

The brief's gem showcase (star) and the quest-line gift; Uncommon, L, tank.

- **A mood and a story in one line**: a fussy swamp cook whose belly bubbles like a cauldron, always cooking for guests who never come. One clear emotion players can quote (the sad-miner meme lesson).
- **Broad, simple surfaces** (belly, bubble blocks) for the gem effects, so it suits the biome's headline serial find.
- **Creepy-cute in the loved sense**: smiling, chubby, a little sad.
- **A gift, not a rare spawn**, so every player owns one and shares the face.

Runner-up: Skipslick, if testing shows players want a mascot catchable on every route.

## 7. Three risks to avoid

1. **Drifting into gross.** Slime, rot, sickly pallor. Keep colours warm, shapes chubby, bubbles opaque and cheerful. If it would not sit on a toy shelf, redesign.
2. **A brown-green blur.** Eight olive-brown creatures at phone size merge into one shape. Enforce one pale or warm patch each, distinct silhouettes (flag tail, gar jaw, tapir snout, frog crouch) and one violet or lime owner part.
3. **Borrowing known swamp icons.** Avoid a quadruped toad with a plant on its back, a glowing mushroom on stalk legs, a witch-hat head, a ghost-bubble grin and the gas-cloud poison blob (roster plan, section 2b). Run every prompt through the originality check.
