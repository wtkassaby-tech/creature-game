# Creature design bible: Tide Flats (Water, Saltwick)

Date: 23 November 2026. Status: [Default] guidance for the Tide Flats scaffold agent. Sources: `00_ROSTER_PLAN.md` section 3, `00_BREEDING_STANDARD.md`, `docs/research/creatures/loved/*.md`, `docs/plans/13_WORLD_DESIGN.md`. `eras/00_SYNTHESIS.md` does not exist yet, so nothing here depends on it. Roster (names, types, plans and roles kept): Glissot, Gulpmaw, Hullock, Cocklemole, Clapshell, Runnelfin, Lanternell, Moatkeep; giant Carrackon.

## 1. Visual identity

**Place.** Saltwick is a fishing town on a wide wet beach: rock pools, sea cliffs, marram dunes, the Old Light, the Heron Causeway to the Mire. The sky is wide and sea-bright, with spray and gulls. The player's first sight of the sea comes from the Stair Gate, so this is where the world turns blue.

**Palette** (flat clay blocks; two main colours plus one accent per creature).

| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main | Sea teal | #2F8F9D | Heads and torsos of most Water species |
| Main | Wet-sand cream | #EBDDBA | Bellies, faces, shell insides |
| Main | Barnacle grey | #8A9399 | Hull plating, rock, the Sand species |
| Main (deep) | Tarred-hull navy | #28455C | Hullock, Carrackon, undersides |
| Accent | Coral pink | #F08A7A | The one accent: gills, cheeks, shell lips, fin edges |
| Accent (one species) | Lamp amber | #F6C25A | Lanternell's lamp only |
| Highlight | Sailcloth white | #F6F1E4 | Sails, foam trim, eye shine |

Rules: one accent per creature, never coral and amber together. Teal leads about half the roster; the other half leads with cream, grey or navy, so the beach does not read as one blue blur. No royal blue or neon aqua (generic, and they fight the matte clay look).

**Materials.** Scallop and cockle shell (matte ridges), barnacle crust (a texture of small bumps, not geometry), sailcloth folds, sea glass (opaque coloured panels only), tarred planks and driftwood, wet sand. Damp but matte: no wet shine, no transparency, no floating spray.

**Shape language.** Domes, scallop fans, hull curves, rounded fins. Outward-bowing curves (hulls, shells) plus one fan or sail shape per creature. Quads sit wide and low; Bipeds are top-heavy with a crest. Against Dust Canyon's stacked slats the coast is smooth and swept; against the Mire's droop it is tidy and taut.

**Motifs.** Shell, hull, sail, lamp, tide line (a wavy band where two colours meet), barnacle dots, rope knots (surface detail only), crenellations. At most two motifs per species.

## 2. Target mix

The loved evidence says cool designs outnumber cute ones in the global top 40, and round plus sleek covers 73% of favourites. The toy and plush evidence (Sanrio, Squishmallows, Jellycat) shows soft, simple, one-trait creatures sell and get collected. A cozy clay game needs both, so Tide Flats leans cool with warmth.

| Mood | Of 8 | Species | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cool | 3 | Runnelfin, Glissot, Clapshell | The beach is energetic. Swept, confident shapes (sail crest, skimmer, show-off brawler) make the poster creatures and follow the pull of sleek silhouettes |
| Cute | 2 | Cocklemole, Gulpmaw | Entry-level collectibles readable at thumbnail size, each with one expressive face (the mood-mascot pattern that spreads as fan art) |
| Majestic | 2 | Hullock, Lanternell | The two Uncommons are the aspirational pieces: size L, calm, a signature that reads from far away. Rarity shows through size and glow |
| Creepy-cute | 1 | Moatkeep | A sandcastle that rebuilds itself is a little eerie and very endearing. One is enough for a cozy biome; the Mire raises the share |

Carrackon is majestic. Spread: about 35% cool, 25% cute, 25% majestic, 15% creepy-cute. No species is only cute: every cute one gets one cool or odd detail (Gulpmaw's mouth is attitude, not a smile).

## 3. Archetypes from the loved evidence that fit here

Principles only; no specific creature is to be copied.

1. **One signature feature grown into the body.** Shell, sail and lamp must be part of the animal, not worn. Cocklemole's helmet reads as a shell it grew; Lanternell's lamp is its head, not a hat. This also keeps each feature on one breeding part (Standard 2.2).
2. **The sleek water-dweller with one dramatic flourish.** Runnelfin's sail crest is the biome's best case: the outline alone sells it.
3. **The old, wise, big one.** Large calm creatures with a history are consistently loved. Hullock carries its years in barnacles and patched planks: a story told through texture.
4. **The light bearer.** Visible rarity on Roblox comes through glow, size or outline. Lanternell's painted amber lamp (painted bright, not glowing) is the biome's one honest light.
5. **One readable mood.** Chunky body, one expression: Gulpmaw is hopeful, Clapshell wants applause.
6. **A want you can see in play.** Glissot cannot stand still, Moatkeep rebuilds, Clapshell wants cheers. Match each gesture to its want.
7. **Fox, wolf, cat and dragon bodies are half the favourites, but the shore does not suit them.** Do not force one. Glissot's lean, fast frame is the only place for a canine or feline read, and it must not resemble any known creature.

## 4. Rare-look (shiny-style) colour rule

A rare look is an alternative texture on the one part that owns the feature (Standard 3.5), so it travels in breeding. For Tide Flats:

**On the signature part only, teal becomes pearl (#E8ECF0) with a navy edge line (#28455C), and the coral accent becomes gold (#F2C14E). The rest of the body keeps its normal coat.**

Why: pearl against a teal-and-sand world reads at a glance on Roblox; the navy edge gives the visible outline; gold is the shared "special" cue across the biome, so players learn it once. It never touches the base palette, so it cannot be confused with a colour-gene tint. Lanternell is the exception that proves the rule: its lamp is already amber, so its rare lamp turns pearl with a gold rim. Other biomes must choose a different pair so two rare looks are never confusable.

## 5. The giant: Carrackon (Water, Quad)

**Concept.** The grand hermit that wears a whole ship. Its shell is a wrecked carrack: three broken masts with scrap sailcloth, barnacled planking, a rope rail along the back, coral growing in the gunwale, a lantern tucked in the stern. Underneath is a soft coral-and-cream body with a blunt, patient face and thick armoured forelimbs. It should read as a hermit crab grown to harbour size, never a monster. Hull and shell sit on the torso and the forelimbs are their own part, so its parts can breed (giants breed with Giant Candy, DECISIONS 21 Nov).

**Fight tell.** Before any big attack Carrackon pulls fully into the hull: the body vanishes, the masts tilt forward, and the stern lantern flares bright for one turn. The next turn it rams or sweeps. The lesson: a retreating giant means a big hit is coming, so guard or swap now. The tell is a shape change plus a bright lamp, readable on a phone with the sound off. The answer is Tide Flats' underdog type, Sand (Moatkeep, Cocklemole), which can punish the ducked turn.

## 6. Mascot candidate: Runnelfin

- **Silhouette.** A sail crest on a small upright dancing body is the most recognisable shape in the roster, readable as a black shape at icon size. Its outline alone says Tide Flats.
- **Personality.** A natural dancer, the one creature visibly happy to be watched. Dance suits a gesture-based game and short looping clips; the evidence says spread comes from one readable mood.
- **Rarity and size.** XS and Common: players meet it early and often, so marketing never teases a rarity. A sprinter, so catching it is fun.
- **Palette.** Teal body, cream belly, coral sail edge: exactly the biome's three colours.

Runner-up: Lanternell, with the strongest rarity signal but a quieter, larger, less cheerful presence. Hullock reads too slowly for a mascot.

## 7. Three risks to avoid

1. **A blue blur.** Eight water creatures in teal and white merge in the Collection and in battle. Split the roster half teal-led, half cream, grey or navy-led (Cocklemole and Moatkeep lean cream and grey, Hullock leans navy) and let silhouettes do the work: domes, fans, hulls, a sail, a lamp.
2. **Costume creatures and borrowed ideas.** Shells, ships and lamps stuck onto an ordinary animal read as worn, and break the breeding contract (features stay inside their own part). Several shore ideas also sit near existing creatures (a clam, a jellyfish, a starfish and a shell-hermit are close to the roster plan's avoid list). Grow each feature from the body, keep it on one part, and run the originality check on every concept, above all Lanternell and Clapshell.
3. **Too cute, too little reward.** An all-soft, all-round biome lacks the cool weight the evidence says players love, and gives the rare look nothing to dramatise. Hold the 3 cool and 2 majestic target, give each creature one flourish, and make Hullock and Lanternell the two boldest models so rarity shows through size and glow, not only a menu label.
