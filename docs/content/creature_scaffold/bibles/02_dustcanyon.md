# Design bible 02: Dust Canyon (Sand, town Kilnrest, 3v3)

Date: 23 November 2026. Status: [Default] design guidance for scaffold agents. Sources: `00_ROSTER_PLAN.md`, `00_BREEDING_STANDARD.md`, `docs/research/creatures/loved/*.md`, `01` to `04` research, `docs/plans/13_WORLD_DESIGN.md`. (`eras/00_SYNTHESIS.md` does not exist yet, so it is not used.)

**Why we invest here.** The loved-creature research rates ground, rock and desert as the thinnest evidence in the genre: Flygon is the only entry in the Japan top 30, and boulder-with-eyes designs are loved as memes, not for design. Dust Canyon cannot coast on its type. It has to win on character faces, one strong silhouette per creature, and colour that reads warm and bright rather than brown.

## 1. Visual identity

**Palette (flat blocks; two main colours plus one accent per creature).**

| Role | Name | Hex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main | Ochre | #D9983A |
| Main | Rust red | #B5482A |
| Main | Sandstone cream | #F0DDB8 |
| Main | Dusty sage | #8FA27A |
| Shade | Baked umber (shadow bands, never outlines) | #7A3E2B |
| Accent | Kiln-glaze turquoise | #1FA8A0 |
| Accent | Poppy coral | #E8553D |
| Accent | Agave yellow-green | #B8CC4A |

The world sits in hot white light with a red haze (the `dust_canyon` climate preset), so creature colours must be more saturated than the ground. Rule: a creature never matches the terrace it stands on. Rust and ochre bodies get a cream or sage second colour; sage bodies get rust. One accent per creature, from the accent row only. Turquoise is reserved for the biome's pottery creatures (Kilnrest fires clay in the canyon).

**Materials.** Fired terracotta (matte, faint fingerprints; the clay-toy house style does double duty), layered sandstone, knapped flint (the one slightly glossy material, always an accent block), dry leaf thatch, waxy matte succulent skin. Nothing metallic, nothing see-through.

**Shape language.** Stacked, layered and slatted. Horizontal strata bands read as sandstone or pottery even at thumbnail size. Spires, blades and shutters break the silhouette vertically against the low canyon wall. Keep the clay-toy roundness underneath: a rounded body with one hard-edged feature on top, not an angular body.

**Motifs.** Strata lines, thumb-pressed pot rims, shutter slats, trail-marker stacking, dust trails, knapped chip scars, a hairline "kiln crack" that glows turquoise on rare creatures. Dust is the biome's effect language: every Sand creature has a dust puff tied to its gesture or a move.

## 2. Target mix across the eight species

Evidence: in the global top 40, cool or majestic is 68% and cute about a third (overlapping); cool wins more often, cute is a durable second, and cute-but-cursed contrast drives sharing. Desert has no cute tradition to lean on, so this is the biome with the most character, not the most cuteness.

| Mood | Count | Species | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cool | 3 | Duneskit, Flintknap, Whirlgrit | Sprinters and the blade-armed fighter carry the genre's most-loved mood. Cool means attitude and one sharp feature, not scary. |
| Majestic | 2 | Spirecap, Wadibuck | The sentinel and the antelope are the dignified, tall, clean silhouettes. |
| Cute | 2 | Thatchling, Spurlet | The huggable commons new players meet first; the plush sellers. |
| Creepy-cute | 1 | Cairnback | A walking pile of stones with a worn, patient face: the cute-but-cursed contrast creature (research 08). A grave marker that is friendly. |

Giant Mesalith is majestic. Six of nine read cool or majestic, matching the polls, while two cute commons keep the biome approachable. Faces follow the research: grumpy, proud, dozy, never blank.

## 3. Archetypes from the loved evidence that fit (principles only)

- **Canid or felid with one colour trick.** The most common animal basis in the polls (11 of 40; dragons 8). Fits the sprinter: a small desert-fox-or-hare body whose one sharp feature (the ears) does all the work, light body with a dark accent.
- **Heavy character with a strong face.** The research's second winning ground trait. Fits the bruiser and tank slots: a face with a joke (grumpy, patient) on a stackable body.
- **One signature feature grown into the body** (the plush rule): ears, capstone, blade forearm, horn. It must be a body part, not an accessory, so it survives breeding, and it must pass the black-silhouette test.
- **The environment is the animal.** Dune, burrow, cactus or rock is the body (the research gimmick). Succulent, mesa and cairn fit directly.
- **Sleek and colourful.** The Flygon lesson: at least one species should be a clean, elegant, saturated shape, not a lump. That is Wadibuck.
- **A job in the biome** (Yo-kai lesson): potters' helpers, trail markers, thatchers. Each species gets one Kilnrest job in its journal.
- **Family shape.** Dust Canyon is the desert version of the Quad and Biped bases other biomes reuse, so keep a shared cue (stacked bands) for the "collect the family" goal.

## 4. The rare-look (shiny-style) colour rule

Roblox reads rarity through glow, size or outline. The rare look here is a **Glaze**: the creature's accent block and strata bands turn to kiln-glaze turquoise #1FA8A0 (cobalt #2F5FB0 where turquoise is already the accent). The main colours stay. The creature gains a glossy surface on exactly one part, the part that owns its signature feature, so it breeds as that part's alternate texture (standard section 3.5) and not as a tint. A hairline glowing crack in the umber shade bands finishes it.

Rules: never change the silhouette; never change more than one main colour; it must read on the red canyon floor from 30 studs, which turquoise does because it is rust's complement. Dust effects on a Glaze creature are cream sparkle instead of tan. The gem tiers (Amber first) stack on top and stay separate.

## 5. The giant: Mesalith

**Concept.** A serene walking mesa (Sand, Biped, XL, weight 90). A flat-topped body in stacked rust, ochre and cream terraces, a small still face set low in the stone, a little garden of agave and sage on its crown, stout pillar legs. Wise and gentle, the opposite of a bully, so the fight reads as respect, not a boss slaying. Silhouette: a table with legs and a tiny face.

**Fight tell.** Before every heavy attack the body settles half a step lower and one cream strata band down the front glows turquoise, brightening through the whole wind-up turn. A Leaf or Water hit cools the band and cancels it (proposal; balance gate decides). Lighter attacks are marked by a small second tell, dust trickling off the crown. Both are large, slow, colour-led and readable from the battle camera by a nine-year-old.

## 6. Mascot candidate: Duneskit

It is the roster's showcase creature, the token gift and the first thing a player meets in the biome. Cool and cute at once (the Umbreon lesson: one colour trick on a simple body): a cocky fox-hare sprinter whose tall shutter-slat ears snap flat when it runs or sulks. The ears are the whole identity, pass the black-silhouette test, and double as a mood display. Cream body, rust ears, one poppy-coral accent. It wins on attitude where a polished but bland design would lose, and the ear snap animates well in a ten-second clip.

## 7. Three risks to avoid

1. **Brown mush.** A canyon of tan creatures on a red floor is invisible. Give every creature a cream or sage second colour, put the accent on the face or feature, and check each silhouette against the rust terrace.
2. **Boulder-with-eyes.** Cairnback, Spirecap and Mesalith can slip into plain rocks with faces, the design the research says is loved only as a meme. Each needs a distinct posture and a joke: Cairnback's patient stack that someone keeps adding pebbles to, Spirecap's careful balance, Mesalith's garden crown.
3. **Reading as an existing creature.** Sand rocks, sleek desert flyers and sand fox bodies are well-trodden. Keep the roster plan's originality check: no sand-red dragon with big green eyes, no rock snake of boulders, no fox-ear shape borrowed from another franchise. Build each from its Kilnrest job and its own signature part.
