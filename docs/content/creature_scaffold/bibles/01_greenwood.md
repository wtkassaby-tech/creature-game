# Design bible 01: Greenwood (Leaf)

Date: 23 November 2026. Status: [Default] guidance for the scaffold agent who owns Greenwood (agent A). Names are placeholders from `00_ROSTER_PLAN.md`. Sources: the roster plan, `00_BREEDING_STANDARD.md`, `docs/research/creatures/` (polls, toys, Roblox hits, viral), and `docs/plans/13_WORLD_DESIGN.md`. The eras synthesis file did not exist when this was written; fold it in when it lands.

Species (9): Mossmitt, Spadesnout, Sloshcalf, Gorsling, Burlhorn, Stumpwarden, Stiltbark, Splashjack, Loamlet. Giant: Hollowbough.

## 1. Visual identity

**Job of the biome.** Greenwood is the baseline every later biome is measured against, so it is the homeliest and warmest place in the game. It must also be the first place a player decides "I want that one", so it cannot be bland. The rule: soft and stubby bodies, but every species has one strong, readable feature.

**Palette (flat blocks, two main colours plus one accent per creature; hexes are the biome pool):**

| Role | Name | Hex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main | Moss green | #7FA650 |
| Main | Bark brown | #8A5E3C |
| Main | Clover cream (belly, light body) | #F3EBD2 |
| Main | River-pebble grey | #A8A89C |
| Accent | Buttercup yellow | #F2C94C |
| Accent | Acorn orange (sparingly) | #D9822B |
| Water species only | Brook teal | #5FA8A0 |
| Sand species only | Riverbank tan | #D8B47A |
| Eyes, claws, small details | Deep root brown | #3B2A1E |

Keep saturation moderate. The brightest colour in any creature is its single accent. No pure white or black bodies; cream and root brown are the light and dark ends.

**Materials (clay reads):** matte plasticine moss, bark with thumbprint grooves, leaf-edge crimping on tufts, smooth river-pebble finishes on hard parts. Wood is carved clay, not realistic bark.

**Shape language:** round, soft and stubby, with leaf tufts and sprouts as the only pointed shapes. Big heads, short thick limbs, wide bases. Every silhouette must pass the black-silhouette test (toys research): a sprout, hump, snout, fringe or stilt must show in solid black.

**Motifs:** sprouts and leaves, acorn caps, clover, gorse blossom, burl and ring grain, river pebbles, lily and reed. Motifs already claimed (roster plan 2c: birch stilts, back pond, moss mitts, burl hump, stump body, spade snout, gorse ears, paddle feet) stay with their owners.

## 2. Target mix across the nine species

Evidence: cool outnumbers cute in the global top 40 (cool 19, cute 13, majestic 8), but cute is a durable second and toys and plush sell on softness. Greenwood is the first hour and mostly Common, so it leans warm and gentle; the cool and majestic ones are the aspiration that keeps people exploring.

| Mood | Count | Species | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cute | 4 | Mossmitt, Sloshcalf, Loamlet, Splashjack | Starters and first catches must be instantly lovable; Loamlet's stuffed cheeks and Splashjack's paddle feet are gags that make fan art |
| Cool | 2 | Gorsling, Spadesnout | The ones a player shows off. A blossom trail and a proud digger stance give attitude without leaving the biome's softness |
| Majestic | 2 | Burlhorn, Stumpwarden | Uncommon and tank-tier elders; broad, calm, tall silhouettes that make the biome feel old |
| Creepy-cute | 1 | Stiltbark | A nervous twig-stilted wader pretending to be a reed. One odd, slightly eerie species gives the forest depth and is the one players will meme |

Result: 44% cute, 22% cool, 22% majestic, 11% creepy-cute. This is more cute than the global top 40 on purpose; later biomes shift toward cool. Give each cute species one hint of attitude (a raised brow, a smirk) so the roster is not one mood.

## 3. Archetypes from the loved evidence that fit here

Principles only, never copies:
- **A canine or feline stance with a plant twist.** Fox, wolf and cat bodies plus dragons are about half the favourites. Greenwood has no predator body, so Gorsling borrows a lean, alert runner stance (long ears, light legs, forward tilt) and Spadesnout a confident, upright digger stance.
- **One signature feature grown into the body.** The plush lesson: the feature is the identity. Fists, back burl, trunk body, ear blossoms. Each sits on one breedable part.
- **Simple dark body plus one bright accent.** The Umbreon lesson: a bark-brown body with a single buttercup accent on one part.
- **A personality or story hook.** The patient stump that plants itself, the nervous reed impostor: behaviour players can stage and joke about.
- **A mascot with a signature idle and a funny state** (viral research), not equal polish across all nine.

## 4. Rare-look colour rule (shiny-style)

Rare looks change one part's texture, not a tint (breeding standard 3.5), so they travel with the part that owns the feature. Greenwood rule: **the rare look swaps the part's main colour to its autumn counterpart and adds a gold-leaf detail.**
- Moss green becomes burnt amber #C8742A; bark brown becomes plum-bark #5A3A4A.
- The accent shifts to bright gold #F7D74A, and the part gains one painted leaf or blossom in that gold.
- Rarity must read on Roblox through size or outline as well as colour: the gold detail covers at least a fifth of the part's visible area, flat and bright, not glowing. Gem tiers (Amber, later Ruby, Diamond) are effects on top and stay separate.
- Never recolour the whole creature, so a player can tell it from a tint-gene variant at a glance.

## 5. The giant: Hollowbough (Leaf, Quad)

**Concept.** The colossal old tree that walks, with owls living in its hollow. The biome's grandfather: a majestic, sorrowful elder driven wild by the tear's leak, not a villain. Silhouette: a gnarled branch crown read as antlers (a stag-tree, so it borrows the majestic archetype without copying any creature), a large dark hollow in the chest with two owl-eye lights, root feet, a moss beard. Bark brown and moss green, owl-eye accent in buttercup. The hollow is the focal point.

**Fight tell.** It telegraphs by rooting: it plants its forefeet (roots visibly burrow into the ground) and the owls turn to face the player and hoot, one hoot per turn of wind-up. While planted it is guarded; the big move lands when the roots lift. This teaches the first lesson of every giant: read a clear wind-up.

## 6. Mascot candidate: Mossmitt

It is the Leaf starter, so every player meets it in the first minutes, and its two huge moss fists are the cleanest signature feature in the biome (passes the silhouette test, easy to draw, easy to stage as a gesture and a meme). The fists sit on the front-limb slot, so the feature is a breedable part players will chase. It reads as one mood: cheerful. Runner-up: Gorsling, if the team wants a cooler face for marketing art.

## 7. Three risks to avoid

1. **Bland-green sameness.** Nine moss-green bodies in one meadow blur together. No more than three species with a green main colour; use bark, cream, tan, teal and pebble grey as mains so thumbnails differ. Check the roster as a row of nine silhouettes and nine swatches.
2. **A cute-only, forgettable roster.** The evidence says cool wins more often. Without Gorsling, Spadesnout and Stiltbark having real attitude, Greenwood becomes a nursery. Give each at least one sharp angle (brows, a pointed ear, a confident stance).
3. **Signature features that break breeding or originality.** Greenwood is the template for breeding: a feature crossing a joint (a moss drape from torso to legs) breaks the part standard, and a lazy leaf-type shape such as a toad with a plant on its back or a glowing mushroom walker reads as an existing creature (roster plan 2b). Keep each signature on one part and name the nearest existing creature for every species.
