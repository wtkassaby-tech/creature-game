# Design bible 09: Arcane (Lanternmoor)

Status: provisional until Ayrton shares his style references. Built from the roster plan (biome 9), the breeding standard, the loved-creature evidence and plan 13 (the Cloud Stair down into a purple cave valley; the Old Lane home to Greenwood). `eras/00_SYNTHESIS.md` did not exist when this was written, so the era research is not used. When the references arrive, palette and motifs move first; the structure (mix, rules, giant tell) should survive.

## 1. Visual identity

**Feeling in one line:** an old fairy-tale book left open in a cave at dusk, handmade from clay. Warm lantern light inside a cool violet world, so the biome is cosy, not gloomy.

**Palette (flat clay colours; each species takes two main colours plus one accent):**

| Role | Name | Hex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ground and big masses | Twilight violet | #4B2E83 |
| Deep shade, cave walls | Ink indigo | #231A4A |
| Lift colour for bellies and faces | Moonmilk cream | #F2E8D0 |
| Life from Greenwood | Moss green | #6E9B4E |
| Accent: light and warmth | Lantern gold | #F2B632 |
| Accent: toadstool and danger | Toadstool red | #C8412F |
| Accent: spark (Storm species only) | Spark blue | #5BB8F0 |
| Stone for trolls | Pebble grey | #8C8798 |

Rule: violet or indigo is a main colour on at most six of ten species. Moss, cream and grey carry the rest, so the valley is not one purple smear. Exactly one accent per creature, and the accent is the signature feature.

**Materials (all must read as clay or handmade toy):** matte clay bodies; rough-pressed clay with thumb marks for stone and bark; glossy glaze for lanterns and runes (painted bright, not glowing); felt-like moss tufts; paper and parchment sculpted in clay for wings and lanterns (opaque coloured panels, never see-through).

**Shape language:** crooked, pointed and leaning, like a storybook drawing. Triangles at the top (horns, ears, caps), heavy rounded bases at the bottom so they sit like solid toys. Big pointed silhouettes, small readable faces. Nothing thin as a whisker: antlers, threads and wisps are thick clay.

**Motifs (two per species at most):** toadstool caps, lanterns, rune marks, open books and pages, bridges and arches, pebbles, crescent moons, hanging moss. Runes are simple pressed marks (circle, triangle, spiral), never readable writing.

**Echo of home:** the Old Lane leads back to Greenwood, so at least three species are Greenwood shapes grown strange (Jackalorn beside Gorsling, Capgnome beside Stumpwarden, Rimefawn beside Sloshcalf): same chunky proportions, wrong colours, one uncanny feature.

## 2. Target mix across the ten species

Evidence: in the global top 40, cool designs outnumber cute ones; fox, wolf, cat bodies and dragons make up about half of favourites; ghost and magic types are over-represented in recent polls; toys sell on a cute face with one edge (Kuromi, Labubu). Arcane is the attitude biome, so the mix leans cool.

| Mood | Count | Species (roster names, provisional) | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cool | 4 | Jackalorn, Runecoil, Sparkowl, Underbridge | Evidence says cool wins. Horned hare, serpent, owl and troll beast give four readable, slightly dangerous silhouettes. |
| Cute | 3 | Capgnome, Rimefawn, Folioth | The collect-and-trade crowd and the 9 to 15 audience need huggable team members. |
| Creepy-cute | 2 | Stringlet, Pebbletroll | A marionette and a freeze-when-looked-at troll cub carry the ghost-adjacent charm: odd, with a round, kind face. |
| Majestic | 1 | Paperlume (rare spawn) | A lit lantern-serpent is the biome's rarity flex; the glow makes it readable across a room. |
| Giant (outside the ten) | 1 | Bridgegrum | The one large, majestic silhouette. |

Why not more cute: players arrive from the storm peaks and should feel a step up in attitude. Why not fewer: three cute species keep the biome sellable and give gentle partners. Creepy-cute is capped at two so the tone never turns grim.

## 3. Archetypes from the loved evidence that fit

Principles only; no copies.
- **One signature feature grown into the body.** Capgnome's cap, Folioth's page wings, Runecoil's carved coils. Never a hat or a held object. Test: remove the feature and the creature loses its identity.
- **Fox, wolf, cat and dragon silhouettes.** The Quads lean on a lithe or noble mammal outline; Runecoil and Paperlume are the dragon family in serpent form: long, clean, readable.
- **Small and loved, large and feared.** Hermit, moth and fawn are the cuddly tier; trolls are the big tier.
- **A gentle face on a big body.** Underbridge and Pebbletroll get soft eyes and a calm brow, as beloved dragons do. Brute body, kind face.
- **Ghost-adjacent mischief.** Stringlet and Pebbletroll carry a smile and a secret.
- **One readable mood per face** (curious, sleepy, wary, proud), plus a personality or story hook each.
- **Two to three colours plus one accent**, tested in greyscale: the silhouette alone should name the species.

## 4. Rare-look (shiny-style) colour rule

Rarity must read on Roblox through glow, size or outline. Rule: **"the lantern lights up."** On a rare look, the accent swaps to one universal biome colour, **Starlight silver-white #E8EEFF**, the signature feature gets a brighter glossy glaze (painted, not actually glowing), and every pressed rune becomes silver, which acts as an enchanted outline. Body colours stay unchanged, so the species is still recognised at once. Lanterns, caps and pages flip from gold or red to silver. One shared colour means players learn the code once, and it foreshadows the Cosmic biome next door.

## 5. The giant: Bridgegrum

**Concept:** a colossal troll whose stooped back is the old stone footbridge over Toadstool Dell. Toadstools, ferns and a single lantern post grow on the bridge; it has carried travellers for centuries and only dislikes being woken. Pebble grey body, moss on the bridge, lantern gold on the post, a few toadstool-red caps.

**Fight tell:** its Hex locks the player's strongest move (fixed effect, no roll). Before it lands, Bridgegrum lowers one arm, a rune on its back lights violet, and the lantern swings toward the player's creature. Rune lit plus lantern pointing means a lock is coming. Counter-play: swap in a creature with a different best move, or bait the Hex with a weaker move. After the lock, the rune dims and the lantern hangs straight; the lantern is the clock.

## 6. Mascot candidate

**Capgnome.** It is the one species that says Arcane in a thumbnail (red-and-cream cap grown from the head, a beard, a calm hermit face). It is small, so it fits cards, icons and UI corners. It is Common, so every player meets one early. It has a hook (a hermit awake for a century and quietly smug about it), and its signature feature is grown, not worn, which the evidence rates as the strongest identity. Runner-up: Jackalorn, if the owner's references lean cool rather than cosy.

## 7. Three risks to avoid

1. **One purple smear.** A violet cave turns every creature into the same blob and kills phone-size reading. Keep the colour cap, use cream and moss lifts, test each design in greyscale at thumbnail size.
2. **Genre clichés that read as someone else's creature.** Wizard hats, owls, gnomes and trolls are archetypes, but a lantern ghost or horned hare can drift toward a known one. Run the originality check on each and let the grown-in signature feature carry the difference.
3. **Fine detail that breaks clay and Meshy.** Runes, book text, thin antlers, strings and lantern frames turn into fiddly, see-through or floating parts and break the breeding standard (nothing crossing a joint). Make runes pressed marks, strings chunky ropes, lanterns opaque panels, with each signature feature on a different part.
