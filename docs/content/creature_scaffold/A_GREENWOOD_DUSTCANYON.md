## How to read a block

Every creature has the five parts asked for (description, appeal, full prompt, short prompt, 3D settings), then the roster plan's extra fields in short form: signature parts, personality, moves, breeding, originality check. Temperaments, cries, entrances, journal facts and favourites of the existing species are in `docs/content/CREATURE_PERSONALITY.md` and are not repeated; only changes are noted.

## Meshy settings for every standard creature (the per-creature notes add to these)

- Concept: Meshy text-to-image or Nano Banana Pro, 1:1, at least 1024 px. **Refine Prompt off.** Attach two approved creatures (the starter trio once approved) as the style reference.
- Image to 3D: **Smart Topology, Meshy T2.5, target 3,000 triangles** (hard limit 3,600), part segmentation on, texture on, matte, PBR off, **Image Enhancement off** for an approved concept.
- Budget per part (standard section 6): head 600-900, torso 600-900, each limb 200-350, tail 200-350.
- Bipeds: **Pose off first**, arms down with a gap, as Ayrton asked for Mossmitt; turn A-Pose on only if the arms come out fused to the body (Blender straightens them, standard step 7).
- Multi-view (Meshy 7.1 High Detail) only where the back matters; marked "multi-view" below.
- Giants: Meshy 7.1 High Detail with multi-view and Auto Split "by parts", 5,000-6,000 triangles (hard limit 7,200), then decimate per part.

## Rules that shaped these designs

These came out of the standard and the pipeline, and changed several old looks:

1. **Everything is mirror-symmetric.** Import step 4 cuts every model at the centre and mirrors the left half, so a one-sided detail (a lopsided ear, one fish, a single side fang) would be lost or doubled. Every charming flaw here is central or paired: a gap between two front teeth, a pebble on the crown, a fish on each side of the pond.
2. **Quad sprinters are fast through feet, not legs.** Gorsling, Duneskit and Wadibuck lose their long hind legs (the standard's 0.40-0.55 L band, front within 5% of hind) and get big feet instead. This also carries the roster plan's leg data fixes.
3. **Tails never touch the ground.** Stumpwarden's prop root, Thatchling's dragging tuft, Spirecap's propping wedge and Mesalith's rubble spill now all point straight back.
4. **Nothing floats, glows or is see-through in the mesh.** Whirlgrit's sand ring becomes a Roblox particle effect, glows are "painted warm, not glowing", and Sloshcalf's pond and Wadibuck's water horns are painted "glassy-looking" (standard section 2.3).
5. **No long necks and no drapes across joints.** Thatchling's thatch and Hollowbough's moss stop well above the legs; Stiltbark keeps its stilts (named stilt exception) but loses its long neck.
6. **Three signatures on three parts**, so a baby shows where it came from.
7. **No real animal named in the prompt more than once**, and "platypus" never (prompt craft: it pulls in real anatomy).

## What the loved Pokémon in these types teach (studied, not copied)

- **Grass starters and route creatures** win on one plant on one part (a bulb, a leaf on the head, a flower collar) on a round, calm body, with a face that shows one mood. The weak ones (many Gen 5 grass lines) stack leaves everywhere and lose the read.
- **Normal and bug route creatures** that people love are "dumb-cute": a vacant, chubby face, buck teeth, stuffed cheeks, a plump bottom. The joke is the face, not the power.
- **Ground and rock desert lines** read through material and shape: stacked boulders, plates, a club, a bone, a drill. The loved ones keep a soft core (a sad face under a skull, a baby face on a boulder) under the hard material.
- **What makes them appealing:** one silhouette feature you could draw from memory, two colours plus one accent, eyes low and wide, and one "uncool" note (a freckle, a vacant stare, a tongue poking out). Our versions of the uncool note: Mossmitt's tooth gap, Spadesnout's mud smudge, Burlhorn's unnoticed horn flower, Spurlet's sunburnt cheeks, Flintknap's tongue, Spirecap's upward glance, Duneskit's sunburnt nose, Loamlet's sand dribble.
- **What we steer away from**, per the roster plan's originality list and our own check: a quad with a plant on its back (Spurlet keeps its rosette low, shingled and blue-grey, never a bulb), a hedgehog with flowers on its back (Gorsling's blossom is only on its ears and tail), a stump ghost with a sprig (Stumpwarden is a stout guardian with a fungus brow), a one-footed clay spinning doll (Whirlgrit has legs and a pear body), a squirrel with stuffed cheeks and a huge tail (Loamlet is a vole with a stub tail and sand pouches), a fennec fox (Duneskit's ears are square slatted shutters), scythe arms (Flintknap's blades are teardrop hand axes), a cactus with maracas or a dancing pineapple duck (Thatchling is a straw-thatched sprite with agave pompoms and no hat).

## The starter trio's family look

The three must read as a set on the pick screen, with three different moods for three kinds of player.

| | Mossmitt (Leaf) | Spadesnout (Sand) | Sloshcalf (Water) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mood | cheerful show-off: a big grin | proud: a smug half-lidded smirk | shy worrier: soft worried brows |
| Coat | moss green | sandy ochre | blue-teal |
| Element on the body | moss coat and moss mitts | sandstone back plates and digging claws | the pond dome on its back |
| The one thing on top of the head | a clover | a balanced pebble | a leaf sprout |
| Shared | the same eye wording, a cream or tan belly, a big round head, chunky short limbs, one small thing balanced on the crown | | |

Rules: same eye wording in all three prompts (the style line's "big glossy cartoon eyes with white highlights"; only brows and mouth differ); three hues a full step apart (green, ochre, teal), so no two starters blur at icon size; each starter's element sits on its body, not only in its colour; each has one thing on top of its head, so a player picking says "the clover one, the pebble one, the sprout one". On the pick screen show them in rest pose with their idle gesture (Bounce, Sniff, Tilt) and Spadesnout's sit-up as its pick-screen pose if it stays four-legged.
