# Structure of successful creature-collecting games

Date: 22 November 2026. Status: research, nothing decided.

Confidence labels used throughout:

- **Sourced**: a page I opened or a search result I read in this session. Links are at the end.
- **Recall**: from my own background knowledge. Not checked here.
- **Inference**: my reasoning from the above.

Most tracker numbers come from search snippets and disagree with each other. Treat any player count as right to within a factor of two. The earlier file `docs/research/market/02_STRUCTURE_AND_COMPETITORS.md` already covers Loomian Legacy's release history, Project Pokemon and Brick Bronze in depth. I do not repeat it. I add the newer Roblox games, the "instant loop" pet games, the off-Roblox games, and the arcade question.

## Summary

1. **The big crowds on Roblox are in "own, hatch, breed, trade" games, not story RPGs.** Adopt Me sits around 400,000 to 500,000 concurrent. Creatures of Sonaria and Dragon Adventures sit at roughly 8,000 to 25,000. Catch And Tame peaked at about 221,000 in March 2026 and now holds about 12,000. Turn-based story RPGs (Loomian Legacy and the rest) sit in the low thousands at best. (Sourced, snippets.)
2. **The newest creature hit is the simplest.** Catch And Tame has no combat. You rope an animal, fill a capture meter, pen it, and it earns money while you are away. The free starter lasso is the first rung of a ladder where better lassos catch rarer animals. This is close to our lasso. (Sourced.)
3. **Turn-based Roblox games have a weak new-player start.** Loomian Legacy: seven starters, a tutorial, then a slow start. Evomon (newer) streamlines this: three starters, a mentor, then straight into the first zone. (Sourced.)
4. **Every long-lived game has a second loop that is not the story.** Temtem has repeatable tuning battles, breeding and trading. Dragon Adventures has lineage-perfecting. Dragon Quest Monsters has repeated fusion. Pokemon has competitive battling (recall). The story is the on-ramp; the second loop is why people stay.
5. **"Arcade feel" comes from structure, not graphics.** Short fights, a quick daily task list, reveal moments, 3v3 quick battles. Pokemon TCG Pocket is the clearest 2024 to 2026 example. I found no creature game that fully copies score-and-rank screens, so that part is mostly my inference.
6. **Daily login rewards are a weak hook; unfinished goals are strong.** One 2026 retention analysis says a nearly finished goal interrupted at session end pulls players back better than a 24-hour reward reset. Egg timers and breeding goals fit this. (Sourced, but a vendor opinion, not hard data.)
7. **Pacing is the commonest complaint everywhere.** Slow openings, grind in the middle (Monster Sanctuary, second half of Dragon Quest Monsters), empty open worlds (Pokemon Scarlet and Violet, Palworld). (Sourced, review snippets.)
8. **An "open" game with a hidden fixed difficulty ramp feels worst.** Scarlet and Violet's free gym order drew criticism because gyms kept fixed levels with no scaling. A clear linear path (ours) avoids this. (Sourced.)
9. **Steady updates beat one big launch, except when the big release is complete.** Palworld fell from 2.1 million (January 2024) to about 50,000 before 1.0, then rebounded to about 920,000 in July 2026 when 1.0 shipped 72 new creatures and full PvP. Catch And Tame ships a numbered update about weekly. (Sourced.)
10. **Our unique pieces are still unique.** I found no game that combines visible part-by-part breeding with a finished story and a lasso. (Inference, matching the earlier file.)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Loop | Catch creatures, put them to work in a base, fight tower and regional bosses, craft from a level-gated tech tree. (Sourced from reviews.) |
| Gating | Tech tree by player level; the main quest sends you to level-fitting regions. |

## Patterns the winners share

1. **A free, instant first success.** Catch And Tame's free lasso, Evomon's three starters and a mentor. Nobody big makes you wait for the first catch. (Sourced.)
2. **A ladder of tools that gates what you can catch.** Better lassos, areas and eggs unlock rarer targets, which gives coins a clear purpose. (Sourced: Catch And Tame, PS99.)
3. **Progress that continues while you are away.** Offline earnings, base tasks, egg timers. (Sourced.)
4. **A second loop after the story.** Breeding for perfect lineages, repeatable battles, trading. (Sourced.)
5. **A visible stream of new content.** Weekly numbered updates (Catch And Tame), 28-day seasons (Dragon Adventures), or one big complete release (Palworld 1.0). Silence hurts (earlier file on Loomian).
6. **Short, repeatable actions beside a long goal.** TCG Pocket's minutes-long dailies sit next to a collection that never finishes. (Sourced.)
7. **Collection with reveals.** Eggs, packs, variants and rare colours. The reveal is the reward. (Sourced.)
8. **Linear and clear beats open and empty.** Critics of Pokemon's and Palworld's open spaces point at empty ground and fixed difficulty. (Sourced.)
9. **Small servers.** Catch And Tame uses 5 per server, Dragon Adventures 20. (Sourced.)

## Arcade style: what makes a creature game feel arcade-like

I found no direct source for this style in creature games, so this section is mostly inference. Evidence I did find: TCG Pocket (fast matches, quick dailies, reveals; sourced), Battle Champion's "3 vs 3 quick battles" (store listing), Bitbeast Arena's minutes-long sessions (alpha prototype page). The rest is design reasoning.

Structural choices that make a creature game feel arcade-like (inference):

- **A battle takes about 60 to 90 seconds**: three or four turns each side, with 3v3 as the default format.
- **A result screen after every fight**: rank (S to C), coins and experience counting up, a "new best" flash.
- **A streak counter** for consecutive wins, with rewards at fixed numbers, and a **one-tap "again"** button.
- **A stage-select map** for repeatable content: a grid of nodes, each with a star rating, a fixed rule, and a boss at the end.
- **Loud, fast feedback**: a brief freeze on big hits, screen shake, popping numbers, a sound for every action.
- **Big graphic menus**: card-style team and creature screens, few words, thumb-sized buttons.
- **A short daily run** (for example five battles) with a bonus on completion.

Risk (sourced from TCG Pocket reviews): arcade timers and caps tend to push spending. Our rules forbid selling random results, so use timers only for fun.

## What this means for our game

Ranked by expected effect. Build effort is my estimate for a small team: Low is days, Medium is weeks, High is a month or more.

1. **Make the lasso a ladder.** Copy: Catch And Tame. Free basic lasso; better lassos (bought with earned coins only) catch rarer creatures. Fits our policy rules. Effort: Low to Medium, mostly data (lasso tiers and which creatures each can catch).
2. **Make breeding the second loop and show each result as a reveal.** Copy: Dragon Quest Monsters (the "next, stronger result" pull) and Dragon Adventures (lineage perfecting). The new body assembles part by part on screen. Effort: Medium. The assembler exists; the reveal and a "family goals" checklist are new.
3. **Leave an open loop at the end of every session.** Copy: the unfinished-goal finding. Show "one more catch for the set" or "egg hatches in 20 minutes" at the end of a battle or route. Effort: Low.
4. **Add a short arcade mode: a challenge board with stage-select nodes.** Copy: the arcade pattern above and TCG Pocket's fast matches. 3v3, fixed rules, star ratings, win streaks, one-tap rematch. This matches the drafted challenge board (approval pack 6). Effort: Medium.
5. **Show a result screen after every battle** with rank, counting-up rewards and a "next" button. Copy: arcade pattern, TCG Pocket's tidy rewards. Effort: Low.
6. **Keep the linear route-town-giant-gate path, and make levels scale predictably.** Copy: classic Pokemon structure, and avoid Scarlet and Violet's open-order-with-fixed-levels problem. Cap NPC tamers the same as the player (Doodle World's complaint). Effort: Low (a design rule).
7. **Fast first minutes: starter, mentor, first lasso within a few minutes.** Copy: Evomon's flow and Catch And Tame's free lasso. Agrees with the earlier file's first-session script. Effort: Low.
8. **Ship a numbered update on a fixed day, with a visible roadmap.** Copy: Catch And Tame (about weekly), Palworld (complete big drops). Effort: High over time, but the format is cheap.
9. **Egg and breeding timers that run while the player is away.** Copy: offline progress in Catch And Tame and Palworld's base tasks, but as timers, not income. Effort: Low to Medium (server-side timestamps and a save field; this touches saving, so it needs Ayrton's approval first).
10. **Keep the creature count modest.** Copy: Cassette Beasts (129 monsters for a full game). Eight per biome at launch, backfilled weekly. Effort: Low (a restraint).
11. **Run 28-day seasons for ranked and challenge rewards.** Copy: Dragon Adventures' 28-day cycle. Rewards are cosmetics and fixed items, never random results sold for Robux. Effort: Medium.
12. **Small servers and a hub where players see each other's creatures.** Copy: Catch And Tame (5 per server), Dragon Adventures (20), Project Pokemon's social park. Effort: Medium.

Rule check: none of the above sells a random result. Recommendation 9 uses timers with no purchase. Recommendation 4 uses game-created rewards, which matches the PvP reward rule in CLAUDE.md. I have not read DECISIONS.md line by line against these.
