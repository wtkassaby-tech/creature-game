# How the Pokemon games are structured, and what to take

Date: 22 November 2026. Status: research, nothing decided. We borrow structure only: never a creature, name, character, move name, text or art.

Labels: **Sourced** = I read it this session (link in the Sources list). **Recall** = from memory, not checked. **Estimate** = my own working from the above.

**How far to trust it.** Bulbapedia blocked every fetch (HTTP 403), so I used PokemonDB pages (fetched), search-result summaries (partly snippets), and memory. Gym levels and the Smogon capture rules are fetched and solid. Route trainer counts, encounter-rate slots, minutes to first gym and Exp. Share history are mostly Recall. A "snippet" below means I only saw a search summary, not the page.

## Summary (half a page)

1. **The skeleton has not changed in 30 years.** Starter, rival fight, first wild catch, a short route with a few trainers, a town, a gym with a leader of two or three creatures at about level 12 to 15, a badge, a reward, a new route. Eight times, then a league. Every newer format (trials, open order, the Wild Area) is a variation on that spine. (Sourced for gyms and trials; Recall for the rest.)
2. **First-badge leaders are tiny and gentle.** In six games I checked, the first leader has 2 or 3 creatures and an ace between level 9 and 15 (Brock 14, Falkner 9, Roxanne 15, Roark 14, Viola 12, Katy 15). Our gate rival (3 creatures, level 12 to 13) is exactly in that band. (Sourced.)
3. **Pokemon's level curve is slow and steady: about 4 to 5 levels per badge.** Ours is +10 per badge. That is double. It is the biggest structural difference I found and the one to watch. (Sourced for gym levels; Estimate for the average.)
4. **The spikes are the problem.** Red/Blue jumps from level 29 (Erika) to 43 (Koga). Players grind there. Pokemon fixed grinding by giving everyone free experience (Exp. Share), and was then criticised for being too easy. A level cap per badge, which we have, gets the benefit without the "too easy" problem. (Sourced for levels and for the criticism.)
5. **Openings are the series' loudest complaint.** Sun/Moon's intro runs about an hour; Sword/Shield, Scarlet/Violet and Legends Z-A are all accused of tutorials that cannot be skipped. Our first battle at minute 0.5 to 2 is far better than all of them. (Snippets.)
6. **Catching is a formula, not a skill, except in Let's Go, GO and Legends Arceus.** Lower health and a status condition help (at most about 3 times from health, 1.5 to 2 times from status), and the ball tier multiplies (1, 1.5, 2). The games that added a throw or a sneak made catching feel better. Our lasso is already in that family. (Sourced.)
7. **Breeding is the long game, and it is tedious in old games.** Walk thousands of steps, reroll, repeat. Newer games cut the walking and the waiting (picnics, sandwiches, no daycare). Our part-by-part breeding is new and has no Pokemon equivalent. (Sourced for picnics; Recall for steps.)
8. **Short formats exist and work.** Unite is 10 minutes (5 quick), Champions averages about 4 minutes a battle against a 5-minute target. Both cut animations, allow surrender and hide or skip fluff. That is the template for our 10 to 20 minute sessions. (Sourced.)
9. **Pokemon is criticised for:** hand-holding, no difficulty, padded open worlds, empty towns, fixed gym levels in an "open" order, forced tutorials, and slow, text-heavy battles. All avoidable. (Sourced.)
10. **Top changes for us:** make the badge reward a move lesson, publish a per-badge shop unlock table, add a third rival beat mid-chapter, justify or smooth the +10 level cap, add half-experience for the bench, and keep every battle under 90 seconds. Full list at the end.

Reading the table:

- **Pokemon climbs 4 to 5 levels per badge.** Ours is 10. If we keep +10, each badge needs about twice the experience of a Pokemon badge, or the cap will always be a wall. (Estimate; see the recommendation on this below.)
- **First leader 9 to 15, last leader 40 to 59.** In total, 8 leaders cover about 40 levels. Ours, over 11 biomes at +10, would cover 100 or more. I do not know our maximum level. If it is 100, the cap hits it at about badge 9. That needs checking.
- **Spikes:** Red/Blue badge 4 to 5 (+14), Diamond/Pearl badge 2 to 3 (+8), X/Y badge 1 to 2 (+13) and 7 to 8 (+11). Players cite these as grind points (Recall, widely reported; I did not fetch a source).
- **Gaps.** Roughly 4 to 7 trainers and 20 to 40 wild fights between gyms (Estimate). Players report grinding before spikes: Red/Blue before Koga, Gen III before the Hoenn Flannery to Norman gap. (Recall.)

### Wild levels by route

Typical wild levels on the route to gym N sit 3 to 6 below the next gym ace (Recall). Greenwood's own: Route A 2 to 6, Route B 6 to 12, gate 12 to 13 (Sourced from our data). That puts our top wild level just below the gate ace, as Pokemon does.

### How Exp. Share changed pacing

| Era | Rule | Label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gen I to V | A held item (Exp. Share) gives half the experience to the holder; others only get it when they fight | Recall |
| Gen VI to VII | Becomes a key item that gives the whole team a share; can be turned on or off (details vary by game) | Recall |
| Gen VIII (Sword/Shield) | Always on, cannot be turned off; whole party gets experience. Producer said most players had it on before. Critics: too easy, you run from fights to avoid being overlevelled | Sourced (snippets) |
| Brilliant Diamond/Shining Pearl | Same always-on rule carried over. Reviewers: not balanced around it | Sourced (snippets) |

Opinion split (Sourced, snippets): it helps new players and evens out a team; it removes choice and challenge. Nuzlocke players use hard level caps by hand, which is close to our rule. (Sourced, a nuzlocke level-caps page appeared in results; not fetched.)

**Obedience rules (Recall).** Creatures above a level threshold ignore you unless you hold the right badge. Gen I to II cap at 10 levels above the last badge for traded creatures; later games cap at 20 to 30 over the earned badge. This is the closest thing Pokemon has to our level cap. Ours is stricter (a hard cap) and cleaner.

| Format | Games | Shape | Liked | Disliked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gym | I to VI, VIII (stadium) | A puzzle or maze, 1 to 4 trainers, then the leader with 2 to 5 creatures | Clear goal, a type theme, a satisfying leader | Same shape every time; some gyms are plain corridors |
| Island trial | VII | A captain (or an NPC), a small puzzle or task, a Totem fight (a boosted creature that calls allies); a Kahuna after each island | Creative trials (the volcano park, the supermarket); uses game mechanics | Totem fights frustrating (flinch-heavy), some trials anticlimactic or too short, no captain in one; too guided (Sourced: Smogon trials article) |

| Open order gym | IX | Eight gyms in any order, each with its own minigame, recommended order only a suggestion | Choice, short puzzle themes | Gym leaders keep fixed levels (15 to 48), so doing them out of order is lopsided; "no level scaling and that sucks" (Sourced: headline only) |
| Noble and alpha fights | Legends Arceus | Rhythm and dodging boss fights with no gym | Fresh | Fewer creature battles |

Rewards per gym (Recall): the badge (raises obedience cap and unlocks a move such as Cut or Surf use), a TM (a move disc, one per gym), money, and the next area unlocks. Teams at the gym: leaders have 2 to 3 creatures at badge 1, 3 to 4 at badges 3 to 5, 4 to 5 near the end. (Sourced above.)

Relevance to us: a gate with a leader of 3 at badge 1 is standard. A move reward per badge is standard. A puzzle before the leader is standard (we have none yet).

## 8. Catching

### The rule in plain English (Sourced: Smogon capture mechanics; Serebii snippet)

- Every species has a **catch rate** from 1 to 255 (Caterpie 255, Snorlax 25, Articuno 3).
- **Health:** lower health makes catching easier. A full-health creature is about one third as easy as one at the last sliver of health. So damage can at most triple the chance.
- **Status:** sleep and freeze double the chance (x2). Paralysis, burn and poison multiply by 1.5.
- **Ball:** Poke Ball x1, Great Ball x1.5, Ultra Ball x2. Master Ball always works. Special balls give x3 to x4 in the right place (net on water or bug, dusk at night, quick on turn one).
- Then the game does four "shake" checks. Fail one and it breaks out. Example from Smogon: a Natu at full health and no status falls to about 33 percent with a Level Ball but 99 percent with a turn-one Quick Ball.
- Gen I used different ball values (Poke 255, Great 200, Ultra 150 in the formula's terms). (Sourced, snippet.)

### Ball tiers and when each is sold (Recall)

Poke Ball at the start; Great Ball about the middle of the first half; Ultra Ball in the second half; Master Ball is a gift (one per game). Special balls come from side shops. We already have three lasso tiers.

## 9. Breeding and creature improvement

### The rules in plain English (mostly Recall; picnic Sourced)

| Topic | Rule | Label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eggs | Two creatures in the same egg group (one male, one female; or one plus a Ditto) produce an egg. Left at a daycare in Gen II to VII; since Scarlet/Violet at a picnic, with a basket that holds about 10 | Sourced (picnic, daycare dropped) |
| Hatching | By distance walked. Gen II to VII: about 255 steps per "egg cycle", most eggs need 5 to 40 cycles (about 1,275 to 10,200 steps). Scarlet/Violet keeps the rule: run, glide or swim on your mount | Recall (cycles), Sourced (rule unchanged) |
| Moves | The child learns the moves it would have at level 1, plus "egg moves" from the father (or both parents in newer games), plus level-up moves the parents share | Recall |
| Nature | Passed on 100 percent if a parent holds an Everstone (a held item) | Recall |
| Stats (IVs) | The child gets a random hidden stat for each of 6 stats, but 3 are copied from parents (5 with a Destiny Knot, Gen VI on) | Recall |
| Ability | Hidden ability passes with a good chance if the mother has it | Recall |
| Shiny | Masuda method (see section 8) | Sourced |
| Breeding speed | Sandwiches with Egg Power raise egg chance (Scarlet/Violet) | Sourced |
| Species | Egg is always the baby of the mother's line (Ditto aside) | Recall |

### What players enjoy and find tedious

- Enjoy (Recall): the egg-in-hand wait, the hatch moment, a perfect-stat creature after many tries, the shiny. These are the "just one more" hooks.
- Tedious (Recall, widely reported): walking steps; breeding a stack of eggs just to roll stats; moving boxes; reroll after reroll.

### How later games cut the tedium

| Tool | What it removes | Label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hyper training and bottle caps (Gen VII onwards) | Needing perfect parent stats | Recall |
| Mints (Gen VIII onwards) | Needing the right nature | Recall |
| Picnic breeding | The daycare trip | Sourced |
| Egg Power sandwiches | Slow egg chance | Sourced |
| Mirror Herb (Gen IX) | Needing a specific father to pass moves | Recall; the search found nothing on it |
| Champions | Removes IVs altogether and cuts the effort-value points from 252 to 64 | Sourced (Hypebeast interview) |

The newer games let you fix the one stat or nature you care about without breeding. For us: any breeding result should be chosen or steered, not only rolled.

### Where we match
Starter choice and counter-pick rival, a free first fight, a first route of 2 to 6 level wild fights, a town with the standard services, a longer second route, an optional side lair, a first leader with 3 creatures at 12 to 13, and a locked next route.

### Where we differ on purpose
- The lasso lesson replaces the Poke Ball tutorial and comes at minute 2 to 4. Pokemon's equivalent arrives at 15 to 20 minutes or later.
- Breeding at minute 8 to 12 (Pokemon's is at the daycare after a gym or two).
- The first badge at 40 to 55 minutes (Pokemon: 60 to 120). Right for a 10 to 20 minute session.
- A rival, not a leader, as the first gate. A reasonable first-chapter choice.
- A hard level cap per badge instead of the obedience rule.

### Where we differ and probably should not
- **No move reward per badge.** Every Pokemon gym gives a move disc. A new move is the best "I got stronger" signal that is not a level.
- **No gate trainers or puzzle.** One tension beat before the leader is the gym's job. We have only a long walk with Corwin.
- **The +10 level cap per badge**, double Pokemon's gym curve. Not wrong, but it needs an experience curve that matches, and we need to know the maximum level.
- **Only two rival fights.** Pokemon uses a third.
- **One hub for the whole biome.** Pokemon puts a healer at each town every 10 to 20 minutes. Route B is 600 studs with no healer; the lair stall is on a side spur. Backtracking to the hub after each fight is the likely failure; our own note says robot players walked to the healer after nearly every battle.
- **No shop stock by badge.** The brief is silent here; Pokemon sells better balls in stages.
