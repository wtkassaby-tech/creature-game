# Legendaries, giants and "first creatures": what players love

Research note, 23 Nov 2026. Evidence is from web searches; polls are noted with their limits. Where a figure could not be confirmed it is marked. Everything here is analysis for original designs, never copies.

## 1. Evidence: what players actually vote for

**Pokemon.** Legendaries rarely win general popularity polls, because they compete against pets and starters, but they dominate "iconic" lists.
- The 2020 Pokemon of the Year global vote (Pokemon Company with Google, 6M+ votes) was won by Greninja with 140,559 votes; Lucario 2nd, Charizard 4th. Press coverage says Rayquaza was the only legendary past the top ten. Pikachu was 19th.
- A "Top 100 in Japan" ranking reported by GameSpot puts Arceus and Mew in the top five and Mewtwo 11th (poll year not stated; verify before quoting).
- Takeaway: legendaries that also read as a character (Mewtwo, Rayquaza, Arceus) rank; most others are respected, not loved.

**Monster Hunter.** Capcom's 20th anniversary "Hunters' Choice" poll (229 monsters, one vote per Capcom ID, results March 2024): 1 Zinogre, 2 Nergigante, 3 Lagiacrus. An earlier Iceborne poll: Zinogre, Safi'jiiva, Velkhana. Zinogre is a lightning wolf-dragon with a clear behaviour (it charges up using Thunderbugs) and a strong theme. Winners are beasts with a personality and a readable tell, not just size.

**Digimon.** Omnimon (Omegamon) tops official-site and Famitsu (2015) reader polls, WarGreymon second, Gallantmon third. Omnimon is a fusion payoff: two beloved partners merge into a white-and-gold knight with a cape. Imperialdramon sits far lower (Ranker community: #46 for Fighter Mode on popularity). Lesson: narrative payoff beats raw spectacle.

**Final Fantasy.** NHK's All Final Fantasy poll (2020, 468,654 votes): Knights of the Round was the top boss/summon, Kefka second, Hades third; Braska's Final Aeon 7th, Bahamut ZERO 8th. A summon won by being a spectacle moment (a ring of knights) tied to a game's best memory.

**Dragon Quest.** A 2010 Square Enix fan survey placed Zoma 4th and Dragonlord 8th among monsters, behind slimes. The mascot is loved; the final boss is respected. A 2021 IT Media poll named Malroth (Sidoh) the hardest final boss; its reputation rests on difficulty and mystery.

**Famous game dragons** (GamesRadar, WatchMojo, Sportskeeda lists; editorial, not votes): Spyro, Alduin, the Ender Dragon, Deathwing, Dark Souls' Hellkite, Ridley, Bahamut, Charizard. Common thread: a signature place, a signature sound or voice, and a fight that teaches the player something.

## 2. What makes a legendary feel special

1. **Scale cues.** Show it against the world (a shadow crossing a town, a tiny player). Scale is felt through relation, not model size alone.
2. **Symmetry and a strong silhouette.** Arceus, Rayquaza, Omnimon and the Knights read at a glance as a flat shadow. Test every design as a black silhouette at thumbnail size.
3. **Halos, rings, wings, crowns.** A ring or floating plates break the body outline and say "divine" without text. Cheap in low-poly: separate floating shapes.
4. **Limited palette plus gold.** Two body colours, one accent, gold or white for sacred parts (Omnimon, Arceus's ring). Fewer colours than normal creatures is the signal.
5. **A cosmic or elemental concept in one sentence.** "The wind that holds up the sky." If it needs a paragraph, it will not be retold.
6. **Mystery and scarcity.** One per world, a rumour first, a sighting before the meeting. Rarity is felt after hearing the name for hours.
7. **Story placement.** Knights of the Round and Zoma live inside a memorable scene. Arrive at an emotional peak, not at a level cap.
8. **Signature pose and move.** One idle, one entrance, one attack animation shared with no one. This is what fan art and merch copy.
9. **Sound.** A unique roar and a short musical sting on appearance; silence before it.
10. **The "one uncool note".** The best designs keep one humble or odd detail (the slime's smile, Mew's baby face). It makes the god approachable and shareable. Without it a design reads as cold.

## 3. Where legendaries disappointed

- **Regigigas**: named a weak design and weak battler; Slow Start halves Speed and Attack for five turns. Lumpy form plus a gimmick that punished the player (Ranker list; French press).
- **Miraidon / Koraidon, Calyrex Shadow, Zacian Crowned**: forum critics say they "went too far", reading as a child's idea of strong, with busy surfaces and robot styling. Over-detail breaks the limited-palette rule.
- **Cresselia, Virizion, Heatran, Silvally**: muddled concepts ("a moon duck with ribbons for legs"); not explainable in one sentence.
- **Too many legendaries**: when every game ships many, scarcity dies (TheGamer and Smogon commentary).
- **Anti-climax fights**: stats with no tell. Monster Hunter's winners have readable patterns.
(Sources are forums and lists, so this is opinion, not consensus. I found nothing specific on Tapu Koko or Type: Null.)

## 4. Principles for our giants, mythics and the first creature

1. **One-sentence concept per giant**, naming a biome force ("the tide that never goes out"). Reject any design that needs two sentences.
2. **Silhouette first.** Approve the black shape before colour; it must be recognisable at phone-icon size.
3. **Palette discipline.** Giants: biome palette plus one accent. Mythics: two colours plus gold. First creature: near-white and gold only, so nothing else looks like it.
4. **Parts that float.** Rings, plates, crowns and tails as separate low-poly pieces; our part-based assembler makes this cheap and gives a distinct silhouette.
5. **Scale by relationship.** A shadow on the terrace, a hill that moves, a town square dwarfed on approach, rather than inflating the model.
6. **Rumour before sight.** Seed the name in NPC lines, ruins and a distant roar one or two routes earlier, so meeting it pays off something heard.
7. **Own sound and entrance** for each: one roar, one motif, one entrance animation.
8. **Payoff for the first creature.** Omnimon and Knights of the Round won through story payoff. The ultimate creature should answer the story's question and tie to the player's own team or journey, not be a stat reward.
9. **Every giant has a tell and a lesson.** Telegraphed attacks that teach a mechanic (type counter, lasso timing); readable beats hard.
10. **Add one uncool note**: a small ridiculous habit or shy idle, so the giant is lovable and meme-able.
11. **Keep scarcity real.** One giant per biome, few mythics, one first creature; never sold, no Robux randomness (project policy).
12. **Avoid the failures**: no gimmick that punishes the player, no surface noise, no concept that needs a paragraph, no mythic sharing a silhouette with another creature.
