# What other creature games and toys teach us

23 November 2026. Research only, nothing decided. Question: how did other creature games and collectible toys build creatures people love and that look like nobody else's, and what does that mean for our clay-toy creatures on Roblox? Read with `docs/BRIEF.md` sections 4 and 12, guardrail 3 (all content original) and `docs/research/games/07_APPEAL.md`.

Tags: *(Sourced)* means a listed page says it. *(Judgement)* is my reading. *(Unverified)* came from a secondary or fan page only.

| **Digimon** | Heavy inks and Western-comic influence (Mignola, McFarlane); edgier, often grotesque. Partner evolves through dramatically different forms *(Sourced)* | Cool factor, and the bond with one partner | The early brief reportedly asked for "like Pokémon"; the designer pushed away from it *(Unverified)* | A clear art voice is what separates a franchise from a clone |
| **Temtem** | Clean, bright, competent | The systems (MMO, co-op, breeding) | Reviewers called many designs generic, lacking Pokémon's simple charm or Digimon's wild imagination, and some looked borrowed *(Sourced, PC Gamer, Escapist)* | Polished but safe designs get the "clone" label even with no copying |

| **Cassette Beasts** | Pixel art; every monster fuses with every other into a new animated form; built in modular pieces *(Sourced)* | Fusions, inspired by fan-made Pokémon fusion art | Little on designs; comparison accepted openly | Proof that a visible, systemic mix of two parents is the hook players already want |

## 2. The "too close to Pokémon" lesson

Three patterns caused the trouble:

1. **Borrowed proportions.** Palworld's controversy was about overlays, not names. When a head-to-body ratio, limb length and pose match, people see a copy even if the colours change. Our body plans (four-legged, two-legged, winged, serpent) must get our own proportions, not the standard Pokémon starter ratio. *(Judgement)*
2. **Generic competence.** Temtem copied nothing and still got called a clone, because safe "elemental animal" designs land on the same answers as Pokémon (fire lizard, water turtle, electric rodent). *(Sourced, Judgement)*
3. **Borrowed content on Roblox.** Brick Bronze shows Roblox will remove a game on a rights holder's notice, taking years of player progress with it. *(Sourced)*

What kept others safe was a single strong voice: Digimon's comic inks, Yo-kai Watch's folklore jokes, Dragon Quest's Toriyama shapes, Cassette Beasts' fusion system. Each could not be mistaken for Pokémon even in silhouette. Ours is already chosen: **painted clay toys with part-based breeding.** No big creature game looks like a shelf of handmade clay figures, and none shows parents' parts on a child the way we do. That is our Digimon-inks. Also avoid names built as a pun plus a common Pokémon suffix, and "evolves into a cooler biped" as the only reward. *(Judgement)*

## 3. What translates to Roblox players

- **Age and taste.** Much of the audience is young, and the all-ages standard applies to our shop. Kids respond to round, soft, funny creatures with a face that shows a feeling (Slime Rancher, Adopt Me!), and also to one creepy-cute edge (Doors, Labubu). A cast with only cute or only cool loses half the players; Yo-kai Watch built for both on purpose. *(Sourced, Judgement)*
- **Phone screens.** Creatures are seen as a thumbnail in a trade window or a battle plate, often at 60 to 100 pixels. Toriyama reportedly held that a monster needs one distinctive feature that survives at small size *(Unverified)*; Doors and Labubu prove it. Fine texture is invisible on a phone; silhouette, a big face and two or three flat colours are what read. *(Judgement)*
- **Trading culture.** On Roblox the creature is also a status object. Adopt Me! Neon glow and Pet Simulator sizes say "rare" from across the server before anyone opens a menu. Rarity has to be **seen on the model**, not only written in a label. *(Sourced, Judgement)*
- **Rarity hype.** Chase variants (Skylanders repaints, secret Smiskis) show a colour change alone can be the most-wanted item, which matches our [Locked] rare colour variations that can be bred onto stronger creatures. But blind-box mechanics are paid randomness: we keep the thrill on free outcomes (wild spawns, breeding paid in earned coins), never on Robux, per hard rule 4. *(Sourced, policy)*
- **Ownership.** Sonaria's palettes, Monster Hunter Stories' eggs and Adopt Me!'s raising make a creature "mine". Ours is lineage: mum's horns, dad's tail, a serial if rare. *(Judgement)*

## 4. Principles for our creatures

1. **One hook per creature.** Each species has a single feature a child could draw from memory: a teacup shell, a candle wick tail, one enormous ear. Everything else stays quiet.
2. **The face carries the love.** Big, simple, expressive eyes and mouth that change with mood (happy, sleepy, cross), readable at thumbnail size.
3. **Toy-first.** Every creature should look like a clay figure you could put on a shelf: chunky, stable, thumbprint-soft forms, no thin spikes that break on a phone. Judge it as a toy before judging it as a fighter.
4. **Our own proportions.** Set head, body and limb ratios per body plan on purpose and check them against Pokémon silhouettes before approving any species.
5. **Ground each species in a job or a joke.** Like Yo-kai, each creature does something in its biome (the one that steals picnic crumbs, the one that keeps the lighthouse lit). It gives names, blurbs, quests and personality for free.
6. **Mix cute with one odd edge.** Most of the roster is cozy; a few in each biome are ugly-cute or a bit spooky, the way Labubu's teeth and Doors' Seek are.
7. **Families, not one-offs.** Like Dragon Quest slimes, a base shape across biomes (a desert version, a frost version) builds recognition and a "collect the family" goal.
8. **Breeding must be visible.** Like largos and Cassette Beasts fusions, a child must show both parents in one look. Part boundaries should be clean and the most distinctive parts (head, tail, wings) the ones that carry over.
9. **Rarity shows on the model.** Gem tiers and rare colours change the look (a glaze, a sparkle, a painted pattern), not just a badge, so value reads in a trade window.
10. **Small themed sets.** Group each biome's roster into sets with a rare member, so completion is a goal and the dex page feels like a shelf of figures.

---

## Rules we take from this

- [ ] Each species has one hook a child can draw from memory, and it reads at 80 pixels.
- [ ] Every new species is checked in silhouette against Pokémon and Palworld, side by side, before approval.
- [ ] Body-plan proportions are our own and documented in `docs/PART_SPEC.md`.
- [ ] Faces are large and expressive with at least three moods.
- [ ] Each species has a job or joke in its biome, written into its blurb.
- [ ] Each biome has a few ugly-cute or spooky-cute creatures beside the cozy ones.
- [ ] No starter triangle of fire lizard, water turtle and grass quadruped; no electric rodent; no ball-shaped capture tool.
- [ ] Rarity and rare colour show on the model itself, visible in a trade window.
- [ ] Breeding results show both parents' most distinctive parts.
- [ ] Rosters come in small themed sets, each with one rare member.
- [ ] Every chase or random thrill sits on free outcomes or earned coins, never on Robux (hard rule 4).
- [ ] Toy test: the model reads as a clay figure on a shelf before it reads as a fighter.
