# Generation 9 (Scarlet / Violet, Paldea and Kitakami) - fan reception

Status: PARTIAL. The shared web-search budget was exhausted before any search ran, so this note rests on a few Wikipedia pages (Pokemon Scarlet and Violet; the Gen IX Pokemon list; Sprigatito; Gimmighoul/Gen 9 species coverage). It has no Bulbapedia, Reddit or poll-site data (Bulbapedia returned 403). Numbers below are only those the sources state. ## Context numbers
- 10M+ copies in 3 days (4.05M in Japan); 27.15M by June 2025; most pre-ordered Pokemon games ever.
- Metacritic 72 (Scarlet) / 71 (Violet), the lowest of any mainline game, and OpenCritic 48% recommend, driven by performance and graphics, not creature design. The design reception stayed stronger than the technical reception, and sales were unaffected.
- About 72 species leaked days before launch; Polygon headlined the leaked designs as possibly "the most divisive yet". Leaks shaped first impressions before the game's own presentation could.

## Most loved
- Sprigatito line (Meowscarada): Famitsu survey made Sprigatito the favourite starter with over 40% of the vote. An Inside Games poll put it second at 36%, and Nintendo Life put it second behind Fuecoco. Meowscarada was third most popular in a GameWith Japan poll and topped a 2023 popularity poll. Reasons: cat cuteness, a slender and "smart" final form that answered the fear of another bulky bipedal evolution (the "Sprigatito Stand" debate), a jealous personality, "Weed Cat" meme.
- Fuecoco: top of the Nintendo Life poll; Inside Games polls cite its vacant expression and cute look. Meme-friendly dopey face.
- Smoliv: grew popular for its anxious personality and its name.
- Tinkatink / Tinkaton: positive community response since debut (small tool-wielding character with attitude).
- Paldean Wooper: praised as cute; nicknamed "Pooper".
- Koraidon and Miraidon: praised for their character and story arc, not just looks (you travel on them).
- Quaxly: instant recognisability via a Donald Duck resemblance, a short-lived meme.

## Mocked or disliked
- Flamigo: called bland. It later became strong in competitive play and popular with speedrunners (power rescued a weak design reputation).
- Wiglett: commented on for phallic look (an accidental-shape lesson).
- Sprigatito's feared bipedal evolution: a worry, not a dislike; fixed by the final design.
- Pawmi: very close to Pikachu, a recurring "mascot clone" complaint.
- Wider coverage (Paradox "robot-ish" designs, Bombirdier, Klawf etc.) could not be verified here.

## Recurring traits of the loved
1. Readable animal base plus one twist (cat with magician flair).
2. A simple, expressive face with personality (vacant, anxious).
3. Cute first stage, then a final form that keeps the vibe.
4. Memorable name that doubles as a joke.
5. Starters win through the evolution line as a whole, not stage 1 alone.
6. Small characters with attitude (Tinkaton).
7. A story role or emotional hook (Koraidon, Miraidon).
8. A nickname or meme spreads a design for free.

## Lessons for ClayPals
- Design the whole growth line (baby to adult) as one promise; show fans where it goes early to calm the "will it get worse" fear.
- Face first: a big, simple, funny face gives meme and merch value. In part-based breeding, keep heads as the personality carrier.
- Test every part combination for accidental silhouettes (Wiglett) and copy-of-mascot look (Pawmi).
- Early leaks and previews drive judgement; ship readable silhouettes that survive low-res screenshots.
- Strength or utility can rescue a plain design (Flamigo) - give plain parts a gameplay role.
- Names and nicknames matter: offer short, punny, nameable parts.
- Technical polish is judged separately; do not let rough builds shape creature perception.
