## Verified facts (source: Wikipedia, Pokemon Legends: Z-A)
- Metacritic: Switch 2 version 78/100 (107 reviews); Switch version 70/100 (9 reviews).
- OpenCritic: 66% of critics recommend the base game (137 reviews); Mega Dimension DLC 56% (18 reviews).
- Famitsu 35/40. IGN 8/10, GameSpot 7/10, Nintendo Life 7/10 (Switch 2), Eurogamer 3/5, VGC 5/5.
- Sales: 5.8 million in week one; 1.48 million in Japan in four days; 13.31 million by 30 Sept 2026 (9.27M Switch, 4.04M Switch 2).
- Battles were the most praised element. Criticism targeted flat visuals, the restrictive Lumiose City setting and shallow side content.
- Design reaction: leaked Mega Starmie (legs) was widely covered. Polygon said fans were "absolutely losing it"; The Escapist ran a mocking piece. Reported Flygon omission from Mega Dimension upset fans (Times of India). Raichu gets two Megas, the third species to do so. Starter Megas were gated behind ranked online play across three seasons, which GamesRadar+ criticised.

## What this suggests (inference, not measured)
- Mega forms work as "the same creature, more": a recognisable base with a bolder silhouette. The reception stories are about silhouette and anatomy (legs) rather than colour.
- Controversy spreads faster than praise: the most-covered design item was a mocked one. A design that reads oddly in a leak thumbnail becomes a meme.
- Fans react strongly to who is included or omitted (Flygon), and to access (paywalled or grind-gated Megas). Obtainability is part of how a design is judged.
- Gameplay quality (battles) carried the game more than visual polish; flat presentation drew complaints even when the creature roster was liked.

## Unverified (from memory, treat as leads only)
