# Generation 5 (Unova) fan reception: notes for ClayPals

Scope: Black/White and Black 2/White 2, the all-new roster of 156 (649 total). Principles only; we copy no creature. Evidence quality is mixed (fan polls, editorial lists); numbers are marked where I could verify them.

## What the evidence says

**Launch was huge, reception of designs was split.** Black/White sold about 2.64 million in Japan in the opening tracking period (Enterbrain), beating Diamond/Pearl's 1.59 million, and passed 5 million in Japan by January 2011. So the roster did not hurt sales. Yet in the 2019-2020 era "Pokemon of the Year" poll, TheGamer reports no Gen 5 creature reached the top 10; the best, Chandelure, placed 17th. The author blamed a split vote (the generation has the most new creatures), a few polarising designs (Trubbish, Vanilluxe, Musharna), and the fact that Black/White hid all older creatures until after the story, so players were forced to use new ones they might not like.

**Polls of Gen 5 favourites (consistent top group):**
- Nintendo Life Gen 5 poll (2,016 votes): Chandelure 8%, Zoroark 5%, Volcarona 5%, Hydreigon 4%, Krookodile 4%, Oshawott 4%, Haxorus 3%, Victini 3%, Excadrill 3%. Trubbish, Golurk, Scolipede, Zorua, Serperior and others at 2%. Readers called Gen 5 "hit-or-miss" and some said designs "started to look over-designed". A staff writer noted "most have come around a bit on Vanilluxe".
- "Every Pokemon is Someone's Favorite" site (over 257,900 votes, per ComicBook.com): Chandelure first "by a pretty wide margin", Oshawott second, over 400 votes behind.
- Editorial lists (Sportskeeda, TheGamer) name Zoroark, Reshiram, Serperior, Hydreigon and Sawsbuck as the well regarded ones.

**Most mocked:** Garbodor/Trubbish (trash bag, called lazy), the Vanillite line especially Vanilluxe (two ice cream cones; "faces nobody could love"), Klink (gears), Musharna. These are literal object or food designs with little animal logic. The generation-wide jab was "they ran out of ideas" (TV Tropes, Bogleech notes the criticism intensified at Gen 5). Defenders (Bogleech, some Smogon posters) argue many designs were fresh reinterpretations (Woobat as a Zubat take, Reuniclus, Ferrothorn, Scrafty, Golurk) and that Gen 5 had cleaner anatomy with fewer decorative markings than Hoenn/Sinnoh.

**Designer intent (Iwata Asks and Nintendo Dream, via secondary write-ups; partly unverified):** Masuda chose an all-new roster so everyone starts equal and spent about a year on how to make designs feel cool to older players; Sugimori was startled at the idea. The team reportedly visited a zoo, and Unova was modelled on New York. Sugimori said "coolness" was a target. Team-size figures (17 designers, about 10 each) come from a partial translation; treat as unverified.

## Design principles we can take

1. The darlings are strong, simple, readable silhouettes with one memorable concept (a haunted candelabra, a fox illusionist, a moth with sun wings, a many-headed dragon). Concept clarity beats detail.
2. Dark/elegant "cool" designs poll highest; cute starters (Oshawott) also rank, because of an easy charm hook (the scalchop).
3. Object designs split opinion. They work when the object has a character story (ghostly candle-lamp, golem) and fail when the creature is merely the object with a face (trash bag, ice cream).
4. Evolution lines matter: final forms are loved, awkward middle stages (Vanillish) drive jokes.
5. Overdesign hurts: too many markings, joints and ornaments read as noise.
6. Roster size dilutes love. 156 new creatures split votes; fewer, stronger designs get remembered.
7. Forcing players onto only-new creatures amplifies dislike of the weak ones. Early-game creatures must be strong.
8. Opinions soften over time: Vanilluxe and Trubbish gained defenders, so memorable weirdness can age into affection.
