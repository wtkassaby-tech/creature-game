# Generation 2 (Johto) fan reception: notes for ClayPals

Scope: Gold/Silver/Crystal fan reception, loved and disliked designs, and what we can learn as design principles. We copy no creature. Most figures below are fan polls (self-selected samples), so treat them as signals, not truth.

## Evidence with numbers

- **Gold/Silver sales:** about 23.1 to 23.7 million copies (VGChartz, Oricon figures via PokeCommunity); over 1.4 million in the first US week (Nintendo, via Animenewsnetwork-era reporting). Crystal brings the family past 29 million (Wikipedia Game Boy list).
- **Nintendo Life Gen 2 poll, 15 Feb 2026, 4,133 votes:** 1 Umbreon 8%; 2 Tyranitar and Lugia 6%; 4 Totodile 5%; 5 Cyndaquil, Typhlosion, Ampharos, Scizor 4%; 9 Espeon, Heracross 3%; Pichu and Togepi 2%. Bottom tier (0 to 0.1%): Ledyba, Pineco, Granbull, Remoraid, Pupitar, Sunkern, Hoppip, Qwilfish. Cleffa and Igglybuff 0.2%, Smoochum 0.1%.
- **Nintendo Life 2019 all-generation poll, 52,725 votes:** Umbreon 6th (607), Typhlosion 12th (534), Ampharos 13th (529), Tyranitar 16th (451). Gen 2 got 9,115 votes, about 17.3% of the total, roughly 91 votes per Pokemon.
- **Official Pokemon of the Year 2020 (Google vote):** Umbreon 5th overall with 67,062 votes, top of Gen 2; Espeon 7th in its generation with 30,052 (Bulbapedia table, via search summary). Eevee family combined was 236,856 votes (SoraNews24).
- **Johto Times 2025 poll:** 582,516 preliminary votes across all generations; I could not retrieve the Johto-only ranking.
- **Gen-level poll:** Johto placed fourth (about 2.2k votes) behind Gen 3 (summary of a fan survey; low confidence).

## Most mocked or ignored (from list articles, so opinion rather than measured)

- Delibird (hard to see a point to the design), Snubbull/Granbull (wrong aesthetics), Smoochum (unnecessary baby of Jynx), Ledyba, Pineco/Forretress (confusing evolution), Sunflora (awkward feet, reuses an existing idea), Qwilfish and Shuckle (ugly or odd faces), Unown (28 forms, one move), Sudowoodo (a tree gimmick), Hitmontop (trying too hard), Dunsparce and Quagsire (unremarkable). Wobbuffet is called an outlier in the natural-inspiration trend.

## Why loved ones work (reasons fans and analysts gave)

- Umbreon: dark typing gives mystery; one strong colour idea (black with glowing rings); readable at tiny sizes.
- Tyranitar: aggressive, powerful presence; also its backstory, being a cut Gen 1 design with a long wait.
- Starters (Totodile, Cyndaquil): cute at stage one, still cool at stage three.
- Lugia and the legendary beasts: more detail and a distinctive shape language than the Gen 1 legendaries, which analysts called ordinary.
- Gen 2 used 3 to 4 main colours with complementary palettes (Togepi, Xatu, Ho-Oh), versus Gen 1's one colour plus accent (Game Developer, "Evolution of Pokemon Designs: Gen 2").
- Ampharos: deliberately placed rings, more designed than natural stripes.
- Steel and Dark were designed in as types (Skarmory, Scizor), so type read through the look.

## Baby creatures

- Babies are simpler, cuter versions of the evolved form, got only by breeding. Pichu and Togepi poll at 2% (tied 11th of ~250), a decent share; Cleffa, Igglybuff, Smoochum, Elekid, Magby sit at 0.1 to 0.2%. Babies that carry personality (Pichu's oversized ears, Togepi's egg shell) did far better than babies that are a shrunken copy of the parent.
- Takeaway for part-based breeding: the baby is a reward moment, so it must look distinct from both parents, not a smaller clone.

## Lessons for ClayPals

1. Make one clear signature (colour, ring, horn) per creature that survives at small size.
2. Give each line a cute stage that is still cool when grown.
3. Design types and parts so role is readable from the look.
4. Avoid object-mimic or pun designs with no animal logic (Sudowoodo, Delibird, Sunflora).
5. Keep filler minimal; 0 to 0.1% creatures were mostly bugs, plants and fish with muted shapes, so make each common creature charming.
6. Babies need their own personality, not scaled copies.
7. Mixing parts must keep the palette to 3 to 4 harmonised colours.
