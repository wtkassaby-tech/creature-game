## Verified evidence (Wikipedia, Pokemon Trading Card Game)
- Pikachu Illustrator is the most valuable Pokemon card ever sold. Only 39 copies exist, given as prizes in a 1997-98 CoroCoro Comic illustration contest; none sold to the public.
- Sales: $5.275M (Logan Paul, July 2021); $16.5M (February 2026); PSA 9 copy $1.40625M at Heritage (March 2026); earlier copies about $208k-$233k (2020) and $772k (2022).
- The card was illustrated by Atsuko Nishida, original designer of Pikachu.
- 1999 First Edition Shadowless Holo Charizard #4 is listed 13th of the 15 rarest/most expensive cards (no figure given).
- Rarity ladder: Holo Rare, Reverse Holo, Half/Full Art, Secret Rare (number above set size), Rainbow Rare.

## Unverified background [recall]
- Mitsuhiro Arita drew the original 1996 set, including Charizard; his painterly, dynamic poses define the "classic" look.
- Atsuko Nishida, Ken Sugimori, Kagemaru Himeno, Narumi Sato, Saboteri, 5ban Graphics, Akira Egawa and others are widely praised.
- Illustration Rare / Special Illustration Rare arrived with Sword & Shield and Scarlet & Violet: the creature is shown in its habitat, often small within a full-card scene, telling a tiny story.
- Umbreon VMAX alt art ("Moonbreon", Evolving Skies, 2021) is a top modern chase card; Eeveelutions and Charizard sell best.

## Design principles (reasoned from the above, to be validated)
1. Scarcity plus a story: the most valuable card is defined by who got it and why, not by power.
2. Creator pedigree matters: the original designer's name raises the value.
3. Habitat scenes with a small creature invite emotion and a sense of life.
4. Chosen favourites (Pikachu, Charizard, Eevee line) dominate: one clear silhouette and a strong personality.
5. Tiered presentation (plain, shiny, full art, secret) lets one creature be collected in layers.
