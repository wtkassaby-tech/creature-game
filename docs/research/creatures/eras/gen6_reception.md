# Gen 6 (X/Y, ORAS, Kalos) fan reception: what to learn

Research date: 2026-11-23. Principles only; nothing here is to be copied. Evidence quality is mixed: several sites (Ranker, Bulbapedia, GameSpot) blocked direct fetch, so some figures come from search summaries and are marked "unverified".

## Scale at launch
- X/Y sold over 4 million units worldwide in the first two days (Oct 12-13, 2013), the first simultaneous worldwide launch in the series, and Nintendo said it was 70%+ ahead of Black 2/White 2 at the same point (Business Wire, Game Informer, Nintendo Life). A retrospective piece cites 16 million+ lifetime (Gfinity).
- Gen 6 added only 72 new species, the fewest of any generation (Game Developer analysis).

## Most loved
- **Greninja (Froakie line, water/dark starter).** Won the 2020 Pokemon Day / Google "Pokemon of the Year" vote, first overall, roughly 140k votes of about 6.6 million (CBR; one outlet prints 149k, so treat as approx). Lucario was second (about 102k). It was also first in a spring 2016 Japanese poll of about 562k participants. Sugimori-era design: ninja/rogue archetype, a frog body with a scarf-like tongue, a clear silhouette.
- **Sylveon.** Sixth overall in the same 2020 vote (Nintendo Life top 10: Greninja, Lucario, Mimikyu, Charizard, Umbreon, Sylveon, Garchomp...). Highest-ranked Eeveelution per a low-authority wiki. Tied to the Pokemon-Amie affection mechanic, so the design and a game feature reinforced each other. Fan art is a large driver (no count found).
- **Mega Charizard X/Y, Mega Gengar, Mega Banette, Mega Altaria.** Game Developer praises Mega redesigns of weak or older Pokemon (Mawile, Kangaskhan, Altaria) as turning overlooked designs into stars.
- **Aegislash, Trevenant.** Called among the most recognisable and best Kalos designs by listicles (Sportskeeda, TechRaptor); sharp, strong silhouettes, a minority in a soft generation.
- **Dedenne.** Unverified: a search summary says it won a Bulbapedia "Pokemon of the Year" result with about 68k votes. Cute small mascot-like electric rodent with a strong fan-art profile.
- Other cited favourites: Hawlucha, Noivern, Goodra and Delphox appear in positive lists; no numbers found.

## Disliked
- **Klefki.** Most consistent target: Ranker fan vote #58 of Gen 6 (440 voters, 46% upvote), described as "poster child" of questionable Fairy designs. A key ring is a concept, not a creature.
- **Vivillon / Spewpa.** Ranker #57 (411 voters, 47% upvote). Criticism aimed at the plain early stage; the pattern gimmick (dozens of regional wing patterns) was liked in concept.
- **Goodra's pre-evolutions (Goomy).** Goodra loved as a payoff; Goomy minimal.
- **Several Mega designs.** Game Developer: Mega Garchomp, Manectric, Medicham, Aerodactyl, Abomasnow, Sharpedo called cluttered, tacky; detail piled on top of an already finished silhouette.
- **Honedge line.** Named in one summary as the most divisive on Ranker (about 69k votes, 4.5k voters, total list). Source not fully verified.

## Why fans reacted this way
1. Simple rounded designs with fewer colours and rounder cartoon eyes dominated the generation (Game Developer: Fletchling, Gogoat, Swirlix, Goodra, Amaura). That made the cute ones broadly approachable, and the few sharp ones (Trevenant, Tyrantrum, Barbaracle) stood out.
2. Starters drew on clear role archetypes (knight, rogue, mage), which made them easy to read and love.
3. Pairing a creature with a mechanic (Sylveon with affection) deepened attachment.
4. Object-creatures with no animal core (Klefki) and gimmick-first designs draw the most mockery.
5. Mega Evolution proved redesign can rescue a weak creature, but also showed that adding detail to a finished silhouette reads as clutter.
6. 3D models made older designs look less saturated; flat clear colour matters.
7. Criticism of the games themselves (too easy, thin story, forgettable villains per Gfinity) did not stop designs becoming loved: creature appeal outlasted the game's weaknesses.

## Lessons for ClayPals
- Silhouette first: a unique outline readable in the 3D clay look at small phone size.
- Give each creature a one-line role or archetype (rogue frog, knight) that sets the parts.
- Tie a creature or a part set to a mechanic (affection, breeding lineage, region).
- Avoid object-first or pun-first creatures; use an animal or recognisable body as the core.
- Keep the first-stage creatures charming, not blank; weak baby forms drew complaint.
- When parts mix, limit added detail and colour count so mixes do not read as cluttered like bad Megas.
- Variant systems (Vivillon patterns, Furfrou styles) are liked as collection hooks, but each variant should look distinct.
- Small mascot-cute creatures plus a few sharp ones gives range.
