# Generation 9 (Paldea / Kitakami): starters and early-route commons

STATUS: partial. The web search budget ran out before any search ran, so only one page was fetched (Wikipedia, List of generation IX Pokemon; Bulbapedia returned 403). Items marked (recall) come from the researcher's memory, not verified this session. Treat all numbers as unconfirmed; re-verify before relying on them.

## Verified this session (Wikipedia list page)
- Sprigatito: popular since reveal; fans nicknamed it "Weed Cat" at first sight. Fans debated whether its evolutions would stay quadrupedal or go bipedal.
- Fuecoco: "significant popularity and praise since its debut, primarily for its appealing design." Inside Games polls found fans liked its vacant, relaxed expression.
- Quaxly: sailor-themed design touches; a brief Donald Duck meme.
- Tinkaton: positive community response; hammer backstory, beats a flying giant (Corviknight) in lore.
- Pawmi: reads as a Pikachu-style electric mascot rodent (a recurring series tradition).
- Fidough: designed by Mari Shimazaki (Bayonetta); later sold as a bread snack.
- Wiglett: noted for an unintentional phallic reading, and for resembling Diglett via convergent evolution.

## Recalled, unverified
- Sprigatito topped many fan "favourite starter" polls and early merchandise sales; Fuecoco became a meme-driven underdog (the "dumb croc" charm, Crocalor/Skeledirge song-and-ghost twist). Quaxly polled lowest at reveal but its final form Quaquaval (dancer) gained fans.
- Early commons that fans embraced: Lechonk (round, pig, "chonk"), Pawmi line, Fidough/Dachsbun (bread dog), Tarountula/Spidops (cute spider), Smoliv/Arboliva, Wiglett/Wugtrio (comedy), Flittle, Capsakid.
- Common criticism: Klawf (upside-down crab), Bellibolt (blob), Squawkabilly (reskinned parrots), Cyclizar (a mount with a plain look), overall "unfinished"/low-effort feel tied to the game's technical state rather than design alone.
- Open-world design let players meet commons constantly, so a common's silhouette and idle behaviour mattered more than stats.

## Design principles (supported by the above)
1. Simple, readable animal base plus one memorable twist (leaf-cat, bread-dog) beats complex detail.
2. Expression and personality (Fuecoco's blank stare) drive attachment and memes more than polish.
3. A strong common (Lechonk, Fidough) is one with a distinct round silhouette and a joke or food association.
4. Evolution that changes pose or role (quadruped to biped, performer, smith) gives players a reason to care about the line.
5. Plain "reskin of an existing animal with a gimmick" is the usual complaint.
6. Designs that invite jokes and fan names spread without marketing.
