# Generation 2 (Gold/Silver/Crystal, Johto): signature designs

Scope: mid-game and signature regional designs. Lessons only; we copy no creature. Evidence is mostly informal (one poll with counts, several opinion lists). Treat rankings as indicative.

## Evidence
- Nintendo Life "favourite Pokemon ever" poll, Gen II winners: Umbreon 7.5%, Lugia 6.02%, Tyranitar 5.85% (generation-winner roundup).
- Nintendo Life 2019 poll, 52,725 total votes (multi-pick): Umbreon rank 6 (607 votes, ~1.15%), Typhlosion 12 (534), Ampharos 13 (529), Tyranitar 16 (451). Four Gen 2 creatures in the overall top 20 from only ~100 new species. Shares are my arithmetic.
- TheGamer argues Johto is the best dex: 27 of 100 new creatures (27%) judged top-tier by the author, versus 23% for Gen 1 and 12% for Gen 5. Subjective, but it names the mid-game cast: Sneasel, Houndour/Houndoom, Heracross, Ampharos, Scizor, Steelix, Skarmory, Kingdra, Azumarill.
- Other lists (Cheat Code, TechRaptor, PokeCommunity, Sportskeeda) repeat Tyranitar, Lugia, Typhlosion, Scizor, Espeon, Ampharos, Steelix.
- Sugimori, 2000 interview (translated, via lavacutcontent): "several hundred" Gen 2 creatures were drawn and only about 100 kept; new types were added for balance; Hoothoot came from a childhood pet bird that stood on one leg.
- Sugimori, 2018-19 (Nintendo Life, Siliconera): tells designers to "take away from designs that are too cool"; adds one deliberate imperfection or cheerful detail so a design is memorable (Oshawott freckles).

## Loved designs (why)
- Umbreon: black body with glowing rings. Two-colour palette, one glow accent, calm shape; the best "cool but gentle" balance.
- Tyranitar: the pseudo-legendary mid-game wall; a heavy armoured silhouette with a fearsome-but-bulky look, reached via an earned three-stage line (Larvitar).
- Typhlosion: a starter whose fire is part of its body (neck flames), readable in a single colour accent.
- Ampharos: an electric sheep with a glowing tail orb; a single light-source focal point; friendly mid-game ally.
- Scizor: a clean redesign of a bug into a red steel-armour silhouette; a strong, simple colour block.
- Steelix, Skarmory, Heracross: strong single-idea animals (iron snake, steel bird, beetle) that are instantly legible at thumbnail size.
- Sneasel, Houndour/Houndoom: sleek, characterful "dark" creatures; the new type got its own visual language (dark fur, sharp claws, glowing eyes).
- Espeon, Azumarill, Crobat: a small number of clear features (gem on forehead, round blue body, four wings) and strong silhouettes.

## Disliked (informal)
- Delibird, Sudowoodo, Sunflora, Quagsire, Forretress, Dunsparce, Shuckle, Qwilfish, Unown (28 forms, weak stats), and the babies Smoochum and Tyrogue. Complaints: a plain object or plant with little personality, "filler" feel, no evolution to build attachment, bland, or an idea over-reused (TheGamer, TechRaptor, Bulbagarden, comicbook.com).
- Babies as a group: the least-loved Gen 2 creature in one fan-ranking was Tyrogue; baby forms added for breeding that merely shrink an existing design read as unnecessary.

## Recurring traits of the loved
1. One-sentence idea (iron snake, armoured dinosaur, glowing-tail sheep).
2. Strong silhouette that survives at thumbnail size.
3. Limited palette: a base colour plus one accent (glow, ring, gem).
4. A single focal feature carrying the personality (tail orb, neck flame, ring markings).
5. Real-animal grounding with a twist, so it feels plausible in a habitat.
6. Visible growth lines: a small cute stage and a big powerful stage (Larvitar to Tyranitar).
7. A type or theme with its own identity (dark, steel) so a new category felt distinct.
8. Rough balance of cute and cool; one deliberate soft or odd detail.

## Lessons for ClayPals
1. Draw many, keep few: Sugimori cut roughly three of four concepts. Budget for a cull before building species.
2. Give each species one focal feature and one accent colour; test at 64px icon size and as a silhouette.
3. Make mid-game creatures the hero cast: rewarding encounters between towns (the Johto mid-game list is where fans' favourites sit), not only starters and legendaries.
4. Give every new type or biome a visual language (material, finish, palette) so categories feel distinct. For clay, vary finish (matte, glossy, glittered, fibre) per type.
5. Avoid "object with a face" filler: if a species is a plant or rock, add behaviour or anatomy that makes it an animal.
6. Babies must add meaning, not just shrink: for part-based breeding, make baby mixes look intentional (shared body proportions, consistent palette rules) and give them a stage that grows into something.
7. Add one deliberate soft imperfection (freckle, mismatched ear) to cool designs; it helps memorability and suits handmade clay.
8. Don't ship gimmick-quantity variants (Unown's 28 forms); fewer strong forms beat many weak ones.
