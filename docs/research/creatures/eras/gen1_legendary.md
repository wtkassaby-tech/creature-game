# Gen 1 (Kanto): legendaries, mythicals, mascots, box-art creatures

Scope: Red/Blue/Green/Yellow, Ken Sugimori's original art. Design principles only; nothing here is to be copied.
Evidence limits: the web search tool returned summaries; several pages (Bulbapedia, GameSpot, GamesRadar) blocked fetches, so some numbers come from search summaries and are marked.

## Evidence with numbers
- Official 2020 Pokemon of the Year vote (Bulbapedia table, via search summary): Charizard about 93,968 votes, Gengar 60,214, Bulbasaur 56,015, Pikachu 48,060, Mewtwo 34,585 (8th among Kanto). Greninja won overall. The box-art dragon beat the mascot.
- 2019 Reddit survey, 52,725 votes (Nintendo Life, GameSpot): Charizard 1,107, Gengar 1,056, Arcanine 923, Bulbasaur 710, Eevee 581, Dragonite 551, Snorlax 433. Gen 1 got 18,289 votes (about 121 per creature); later generations fell steadily, Gen 7 about 20 per creature. Nostalgia and first-exposure matter.
- 2021 Japanese Twitter poll (Game Rant): Pikachu 7th, Charizard 18th. Taste differs by region; Japan favours rounder, cuter designs.
- Mew: programmer Shigeki Morimoto slipped it in late using space freed by removing debug code; Nintendo reportedly did not know. After players found it, a promotion drew over 78,000 entrants and is credited with a sales boost (Wikipedia, Dexerto, GameSpot "How Mew saved Pokemon").
- Competitive view (Smogon "The Original Legendaries"): Mewtwo called the best Gen 1 creature and banned from normal play; Mew also banned. Of the birds, Zapdos best, Articuno most walled, Moltres hampered by weak Fire typing and thin movepool.

## Design statements
- Sugimori (Nintendo Life, 2018): designs must be memorable; "too cool" designs get something uncool added, a cheerful feature added to serious ones (the Oshawott freckles example).
- Sugimori on Charmander (Pokemon.com profile): made so that Charizard is unimaginable, making evolution a surprise.
- Mewtwo was one of the first designs made, before Pikachu (Wikipedia).

## Which landed
- Charizard: top Kanto vote in two large polls; a dragon silhouette with flame tail, reached through a surprising evolution, and was the box-art face.
- Gengar, Arcanine, Eevee, Dragonite, Snorlax: strong polls without being legendary. Readable shapes and personality beat rarity.
- Mewtwo: respected rather than adored (8th Kanto); a humanoid, cold, sleek design with a strong backstory. Strong because of the story and power fantasy.
- Mew: its appeal is the secret and the chase, more than the cute pink body.
- Pikachu: mascot via simplicity and expressive face; good in the West, mid in Japan polls.

## Which disappointed
- Legendary birds: praised for colour and silhouette (Zapdos jagged and yellow) but are recolours of one bird frame, and play weakly (walled by common creatures). Rare does not equal powerful.
- Moltres: weakest in play and least loved of the trio.

## Recurring traits of loved designs
1. One-glance silhouette and one signature colour.
2. A feature you can name from memory (flame tail, big ears, sleepy belly).
3. Animal or myth anchor plus one twist.
4. Evolution or transformation payoff.
5. Chunky proportions, big head or body, readable at tiny size (pixel sprites).
6. Personality in pose or face, not only power.
7. Aspirational "cool" balanced with something soft.
8. Story or secret around it (Mew).

## Lessons for ClayPals
1. Make special creatures special through mechanism and story, not only a rarer recolour; the birds show shared-template legendaries feel thin.
2. Silhouette plus one colour must work at phone size; test as black shapes.
3. Give each rare design a nameable signature part. In part-based breeding, make signature parts (tail, head) the thing players chase and show off.
4. Add one deliberately soft or odd detail to cool designs (Sugimori's rule); clay-toy style suits it.
5. Surprise reveals (growth stage, baby mix) generate talk; Charmander to Charizard is the model.
6. Rarity needs matching usefulness; a rare creature that loses to common ones disappoints.
7. Secrets and discovery drive community buzz (Mew). Consider a hidden, earned, never-paid mystery creature.
8. Popularity skews to early exposure and starters; put the best-designed creatures in the first biome.
