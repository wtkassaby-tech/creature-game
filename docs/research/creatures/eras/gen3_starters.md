# Gen 3 (Hoenn): starters and early-route creatures

Scope: Ruby, Sapphire, Emerald, FireRed/LeafGreen. Lessons only; no creature is to be copied.

## Evidence

**Nintendo Life poll, favourite Hoenn Pokemon, 2,424 votes** (self-selected readers, so nostalgia-heavy).
Top: Rayquaza 9%, Gardevoir 7%, Mudkip 6%, Blaziken 5%, Flygon 5%, Swampert 4%, Absol 4%, Metagross 4%, Sceptile 3%, Milotic 3%, Aggron 3%.
Starter stage 1s and mids are near zero (Treecko 1%, Torchic 1%, Combusken 0.2%, Marshtomp 0%). Mudkip is the exception.
Early commons barely register: Zigzagoon 0.9%, Mightyena 0.7%, Poochyena 0.4%, Wurmple 0.2%, Taillow 0%, Ralts 0.7%.
Voter comments: Spheal "peak design" (round); Numel "adorably dopey face"; Absol edgy; Tropius funny banana neck; Flygon for the design.

**SVG poll, best starter generation, 618 US gamers:** Gen 1 34.6%, Gen 3 18.9% (second), Gen 2 15.2%, Gen 4 11.3%. The article attributes this partly to nostalgia.

**Nintendo Life starters poll (2020):** Torchic took 3rd in Fire with 13%. A commenter noted Gen 3 took third place in each type, suggesting Hoenn starters sit in a strong second tier behind Gen 1 and 2 on familiarity.

**Pokemon of the Year (Bulbapedia, Hoenn table, per search snippet; page itself blocked, year not confirmed):** Rayquaza 60,939 votes, Gardevoir 60,596, then Flygon, Sceptile, Blaziken. Treat as indicative only.

**Design interviews (secondary):** Nintendo Life (25 Jul 2022, sourced from a YouTube channel, "reportedly") says Sugimori designed Blaziken first for Hoenn as a deliberate test of whether players would accept a more humanoid creature, and that Torchic was inspired by painted chicks that "start out cute then grow into something fierce." Reader comments split: some love Blaziken, some find it too humanoid. A 2004 CGWorld quote about mudskippers inspiring Mudkip comes from a secondary site and is unverified.

**Mudkip meme (Bulbanews, KnowYourMeme, via search):** "so I herd u liek mudkipz" and creepy Marshtomp spread widely; Mudkip returned in Pokemon Go era. Meme reach turned a mid-popularity design into a cultural touchstone, but IGN opined Mudkip's later forms are not special.

**Gamedeveloper.com Gen 3 design series:** Gen 3 reuses motifs (eye rings, zigzags, circles filling blank space), bold contrast palettes (red/blue, blue/orange) enabled by GBA colour, and ability-driven forms (Castform, Kecleon, Slaking). Early commons mirror Gen 2 slots: Zigzagoon/Linoone vs Sentret/Furret, Taillow/Swellow vs Hoothoot/Noctowl, Wurmple a Caterpie/Weedle blend.

**TheGamer:** Wurmple line called weak in battle; "bad design" list for Gen 3: Seedot (plain sphere), Nosepass, Swalot, Spoink, Spinda, Whiscash (unsettling eyes), Luvdisc (looks unfinished), Shelgon (unattractive middle stage).

## Findings

1. Loved Hoenn creatures are mostly final forms or one-stage standouts; middle stages are forgotten. Players love the cute start and the cool end.
2. Clear silhouette plus one memorable feature (Mudkip's fin and cheeks, Gardevoir's gown, Absol's blade horn, Flygon's wing shape) beats complexity.
3. Round, dopey faces win affection even for commons (Numel, Spheal).
4. Commons are loved through use and nostalgia (Zigzagoon is the first catch, so memory does the work), not through poll strength. No quantitative evidence says commons are beloved; they are mostly ignored. Evidence gap noted.
5. Weak middle stages and "unfinished" shapes (Shelgon, Luvdisc, Seedot) are what fans call filler.
