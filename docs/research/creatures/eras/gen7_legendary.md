# Gen 7 (Sun/Moon, USUM, Let's Go): legendaries, mascots, special creatures

Scope: how Alola's legendaries, Ultra Beasts, regional forms and mascots were made to feel special, and what landed. Evidence quality is mixed; gaps are flagged.

## What the evidence says

**Numbers are thin for legendaries.** The Nintendo Life 2019 poll (52,000+ voters, all 809 Pokemon) gave Gen 7 only 1,776 votes in total, the lowest average per Pokemon (20.2) of any generation, with no Gen 7 Pokemon in the top 20 and Yungoos on zero votes. Gen 7 as a whole was a weak generation for raw popularity, so its winners are interesting precisely because they broke through.

**Mimikyu is the clear fan favourite.** ComicBook.com's fan-vote roundup names it Gen 7's top pick; a Smogon forum thread calls it a clear winner over Lycanroc and Rowlet. Kotaku's Patricia Hernandez called it the "most brilliant and heartbreaking" Pokemon, and The Pokemon Company gave it a promotional month (October 2019) and Japan merchandise from September 2018. Its hook is story: a lonely creature wears a Pikachu costume because Pikachu merchandise is loved; its true form is said to be horrifying. Design principle: one readable silhouette plus a secret.

**Solgaleo and Lunala (box art).** Smogon's design review praises Solgaleo's mane, white/red/gold palette and starry forehead, and Lunala's night-sky forehead, wings, rib cage and unmistakable silhouette. Criticisms: Solgaleo reads as a generic "anime lion" echoing Pyroar and Metagross, and the fiery look clashes with Psychic/Steel; Lunala resembles Yveltal, is clutter-prone (too many crescents and tassels) and has an empty chest. Lesson: a strong celestial motif (sun vs moon, shared forehead mark) sells the pair, but borrowing familiar shapes cost originality points.

**Necrozma.** Reception is mixed; Ultra Necrozma is generally judged the best (a six-winged, seraph-like form, praised alongside its battle theme), while base Necrozma is called "abstract to the point of forgettable" (secondary, Wikipedia-derived draft; treat cautiously). The final form was chosen for "complexity of design".

**Ultra Beasts.** Game Developer's retrospective notes they deliberately break Pokemon rules (no faces on Nihilego, Kartana, Xurkitree; odd materials like paper and glass) so they feel alien, and the author calls them one-offs and wishes Nihilego were catchable. Yusuke Ohmura said they drew on his designs Sugimori previously rejected (reported by VGC, Kotaku, Nintendo Life, Feb 2026); Sugimori said they are intentionally contradictory. Critics found Buzzwole a let-down and Pheromosa uncomfortable; some fans felt they "never felt like Pokemon".

**Regional forms.** Game Developer: Alolan forms either soften the original (Raichu, Ninetales, Persian) or add detail (Dugtrio, Golem, Muk, Marowak). Island isolation gave an in-world reason for variation. I found no vote data in this pass.

**Sugimori's design philosophy.** Balance cool with uncool: add something goofy to an over-cool design, something cheerful to a grim one (Nintendo Everything / Siliconera). Destructoid reported an art director saying "memorable" beats "cool" (page blocked, headline only).

**Not found:** Tapu guardians reception, Zeraora/Marshadow/Magearna reception, Let's Go-specific data. Search returned nothing usable.

## Lessons for ClayPals
1. Hook plus silhouette: one sentence of story and one shape readable at thumbnail size.
2. Theme pairs (sun/moon) need contrasting silhouettes, not just palette swaps.
3. Ornament budget: too many motifs (Lunala) clutters; in clay, fewer chunky features read better.
4. Legendaries need original anatomy, not "familiar animal plus glow".
5. Alien designs divide; weirdness should be inviting, not uncomfortable.
6. Variant forms with an ecological reason feel earned.
7. Give special creatures a visibly more complex final form.
