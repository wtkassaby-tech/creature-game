## Verified from Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Go)
- Community Day launched 20 Jan 2018: monthly event raising spawns of one featured creature. Fully evolving it during the event grants an exclusive move (first: Pikachu/Raichu with Surf).
- Shinies appear by random encounter or boosted on Community Days. Many players aim to complete the Pokedex.
- Buddy system (Sept 2016): pick one creature to walk beside your avatar; it earns Candy by distance, with a per-species distance requirement.
- Revenue: about $950M in 2016 (App Annie), $1.3B in 2018, $1.92B in 2020 (SuperData), about $4.2B cumulative by Nov 2020 (Sensor Tower), $641.6M in H1 2021.
- Monetisation is items (Incense, Lure Modules, Lucky Eggs) bought with PokeCoins; catching is a flick-throw with timing and ball type; Candy and Stardust fuel growth.

## Background (unverified here)
- Demand clusters on: starter-line and mascot creatures (Pikachu, Eevee evolutions, Charmander line), cute small creatures as buddies, and strong "dragon/pseudo-legendary" lines (Dratini, Larvitar, Bagon, Gible, Beldum) because Community Day makes them obtainable plus a signature move.
- Shiny hunting is driven by visible colour change, so strongly coloured, distinct-silhouette creatures are most hunted.
- Event design (one featured creature, boosted spawns, 3-hour window) creates ritual and social sharing.

## Design principles that survive without numbers
1. Phone screens are small: silhouette and one or two bold colours must read at thumbnail size.
2. Cute-and-small wins as a companion (buddy); you look at it every session.
3. Ownership signals matter: shiny, size, exclusive move, and evolution are all ways to show off.
4. Featuring one creature at a time concentrates attention and creates hype.
5. Recolour variants are a strong collecting hook when the base colour scheme is simple and recognisable.
6. Walking companion = identity; players pick the buddy that says something about them.
