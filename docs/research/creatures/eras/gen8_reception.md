# Generation 8 reception (Galar, Hisui) - notes for ClayPals

Status: PARTIAL. The web search budget for this run was exhausted before any search ran, so everything below comes from Wikipedia pages fetched directly. Polls with vote counts, Reddit threads, designer interviews and sales-by-creature data were NOT reached. Re-run with search access to fill the gaps. Items marked (unverified) are from memory and must be checked.

## Evidence gathered

Sales and context (Wikipedia, Sword and Shield; Legends: Arceus):
- Sword/Shield: 6M+ worldwide in the opening weekend, about 26.7M by May 2025 (third best-selling in the series). Metacritic 80, OpenCritic 65% recommend.
- Legends: Arceus: 6.5M in week one, 14.83M by March 2023, Metacritic 83. Reviewers praised wild Pokemon roaming and animation; one reviewer (Ars Technica) disliked the graphics and the small number of new species (7 new species, 17 new forms).
- The generation's biggest fan anger was "Dexit" (cut national Pokedex, #BringBackNationalDex), not the creature designs. Model reuse claims drove review-bombing (#GameFreakLied). Lesson: the creatures were mostly judged on charm; the anger was about what was removed.

Loved designs and evidence:
- Wooloo: Polygon called it "perfect in every way". Director Shigeru Ohmori said its popularity surprised him; he saw it as "just another Pokemon in the mix". Tumblr's 2019 most-discussed list put it 2nd, behind Pikachu. Large fan art and meme volume; plush, figures, sweaters, Funko Pop; PETA's use of its image caused backlash against PETA. Design origin: team noticed sheep are everywhere in Britain. Round white wool, dark face and hooves, bounces off blows.
- Corviknight: became a fan favourite right after its 5 June 2019 reveal. Based on the Tower of London ravens (real-world myth hook). Armoured bird silhouette.
- Yamper: critics and fans called it adorable; possible nod to the Queen's corgis.
- Alcremie: praised as cute (the page flags this attribution as weakly sourced). Also drew jokes about innuendo (Eater, Time).
- Galarian Corsola: critics read it as a coral-bleaching allegory and praised the message.
- Dracovish: popular in competitive play thanks to move and stats (utility, not looks).
- Starter poll (official Pokemon Twitter): Sobble 38%, Scorbunny 37%, Grookey 25%. Small gaps; every starter had a clear fan base.
- Hisuian Zorua/Zoroark: a Hisuian Snow web series featuring Hisuian Zorua won a Webby People's Voice award (cute plus story). Hisuian forms are described as ghost-flame Typhlosion, poisonous solitary Sneasel (design tells a story).

Criticised or mocked:
- Impidimp: Game Informer's Ben Reeves called it a "monstrosity".
- Rolycoly: Game Revolution's David Lozada called its backstory "depressing" (a rock used as fuel, compared to slavery) and listed it among the weirdest Pokemon.
- Alcremie: also listed as "weirdest" by Lozada, jokes about its sentient-cream lore.
- Obstagoon: noted for resemblance to Gene Simmons of Kiss (he called it flattering). Meme fodder but harmless.
- (unverified) Everyday-object designs such as Trubbish, Stonjourner, Falinks, Sirfetch'd and Copperajah were widely joked about; no numbers gathered.

## Patterns
1. The breakout loved creatures are the simplest shapes: a round sheep, a corgi-like pup. One strong silhouette, one or two colours.
2. A real-world hook people already know (Britain's sheep, Tower ravens, corgis) makes a creature feel instantly familiar.
3. Vulnerable or anxious personality (Wooloo) invites protection and meme-making.
4. Armoured, cool designs also win, so loved is not only cute.
5. A design that carries a message or story (Corsola, Hisuian forms) earns press and affection.
6. Mockery goes to creatures whose concept is an object or whose lore is dark or crude; mockery still produced attention.
7. Regional variants work when they change the story, not only the colours.

## Lessons for ClayPals
- Make a few creatures near-mascot simple: readable at thumbnail size, soft round clay forms, and lean into a vulnerable expression.
- Give each species a one-line real-world hook and a bit of lore; written lore drives press and memes (good and bad), so check it for tone.
- Part-based breeding must keep silhouettes legible: limit parts to a few chunky shapes so mixed babies still look intentional.
- Avoid inanimate-object or gross concepts unless the clay style makes them charming.
- Plan a shareable-moment feature (meme-friendly reactions) because the most loved creature lived through fan art, not stats.
- Keep what players already have: backlash was about removed content, so never cut owned creatures or parts.
