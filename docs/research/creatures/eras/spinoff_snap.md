## What the games are (recalled, high confidence)

- Pokemon Snap (Nintendo 64, 1999, HAL Laboratory): an on-rails photo ride. Professor Oak scores each
  photo on pose, size, centring, number of subjects and rarity of the moment. Many creatures only act
  in a special way when you use a tool (apples, Pester Balls, Poke Flute) or when something in the
  level triggers them.
- New Pokemon Snap (Switch, 2021, Bandai Namco Studios): the same loop with many more creatures per
  course, day and night variants, and "Illumina" glowing creatures that react to a scan and an orb.
  Photos are rated in stars, and a course is revisited to find new behaviours.

## Design observations (recalled, medium confidence)

1. Creatures are loved for what they DO. A common-looking creature performing a rare behaviour
   (surfing, sleeping on a path, dancing, being startled) is remembered more than a static rare one.
2. Behaviours are staged by the player: throw food, play a sound, scan, wait. Cause and effect on a
   creature is the reward, and it makes the creature feel alive and reactive.
3. Comedy and cuteness come from readable silhouettes and exaggerated, short animation loops
   (a splash, a stumble, a yawn, a startled hop).
4. Environment is part of the creature's identity: where it lives, what it eats, and what it does
   there tells its story without text.
5. Variety of behaviour per creature (several photographable moments) gives repeat value.
6. Players share photos. A moment that reads in one still frame spreads.

## Disliked patterns (recalled, low confidence)

- Creatures with only one pose or no reaction felt like scenery.
- Behaviours hidden behind unclear triggers frustrated players.
- Very small or far-away creatures were hard to appreciate in a photo.

## Lessons for ClayPals

See the structured result returned with this note. Short form: give each species two or three idle
loops and one reaction, make them readable at phone size, show creatures doing something in their
biome, and let hybrid babies inherit a behaviour from a parent part so breeding changes how a
creature acts and not only how it looks.
