# Generation 5 (Unova): starters and early commons

Research notes for ClayPals. Design principles only; we copy no creature. Evidence is mostly fan polls (self-selected), so treat numbers as direction, not truth.

## Context: why Gen 5 is a useful case
- Black/White used only new creatures (156) so everyone "started on the same page" (Masuda). Sugimori: an all-new ecosystem let the team "return to square one" and study real animals again. About 17 designers drew roughly 10 each; they designed about 3x more than were used; selection balanced "ferocious enough for battle" with "endearing enough to be a companion".
- Goal was to look "cool" and appeal to older players: sharper, angular lines. The cost: the generation reads as less cuddly, and critics say Game Freak "ran out of ideas" on objects and palette-swap sets.

## Official vote numbers (Pokemon of the Year, 2020, ~Google/Pokemon Company)
Unova leaders: Chandelure 50,943; Zoroark 43,782; Hydreigon 40,054; Serperior 22,269 (6th in Gen 5); Zorua 14,910; Samurott 18th in Gen 5 at 11,444. (Via search summary of Bulbapedia; the page itself blocked fetching.) For scale, Lucario was 2nd overall at 102,259.

## Nintendo Life poll (2,016 votes, all Unova)
Chandelure 8%, Zoroark 5%, Volcarona 5%, Hydreigon 4%, Krookodile 4%, Oshawott 4%, Haxorus 3%, Excadrill 3%, Victini 3%; Snivy, Tepig, Samurott, Serperior, Zorua, Trubbish, Golurk ~2%.

## Starters
- Snivy won a 2015 bracket (42.15%), Oshawott a hair behind (41.9%), Tepig distant (15.95%). In the Nintendo Life poll Oshawott led the three at 4%. Ranker (fan list) had Serperior 9th, Samurott 10th (1,829 votes), Emboar 57th (974 votes).
- Pattern: the sleek, elegant final (Serperior) and the clean "costume" final (Samurott: samurai) beat the heavy, brawny one (Emboar). The smallest, cutest first stage (Oshawott) pulls the most affection.
- Each final is a different culture archetype (European noble, Chinese warlord, Japanese samurai): strong one-line identity.
- Gen 5 starters rank low as a generation (4.37% of generation-level votes), partly because Gen 5 was polarising.

## Early-route commons
Little hard data exists. Forum evidence (PokeCommunity "early game of Black/White"): early mons are "fine" but few; Lillipup is the one named as fun and used often, even though Herdier is dull; Patrat and Purrloin are seen as useful early then dropped. Audino was designed to fill a role (nurse assistant) and is widely liked as a friendly, pink, soft shape. Roggenrola (rock with a glowing core) and Woobat replaced Geodude/Zubat as cave commons. Pidove is the most common encounter (about 40% on Route 3), a pure filler bird. Verdict: commons that are loved have a face and a personality hook (loyal puppy, thieving cat); commons that are filler are plain animals (squirrel, pigeon).

## Disliked (critics and fan lists)
Basculin ("worst design", a fish with two barely different forms), Vanillite line (ice cream), Trubbish/Garbodor (rubbish), Stunfisk, Klinklang (gears), the Pansage/Pansear/Panpour monkeys (palette swaps), the forces trio recolours, Kyurem Black/White (cluttered). Note Trubbish still polls ~2%: weird ideas divide, not always fail.

## Recurring traits of the loved
1. One strong silhouette plus one signature feature (Zoroark's mane, Chandelure's flames, Volcarona's wings).
2. Evolution that adds drama without losing the base identity (Zorua to Zoroark).
3. Dark or fiery palettes with a single bright accent.
4. A clear role fantasy (illusionist, samurai, dragon).
5. Cute first stage, impressive final.
6. Costume/culture archetype that reads instantly.

## Lessons for ClayPals
- Give every part (head, body, legs, tail) one readable job so mixes still read.
- Design the first stage for cuteness, the finals for a silhouette.
- Commons need a personality hook, not just an animal.
- Avoid recolour sets and everyday objects without a face; object designs need a charm hook.
- Overly busy designs (Kyurem) fail; keep part count and colour count low, which suits clay.
- Make mixed parts keep palette harmony.
