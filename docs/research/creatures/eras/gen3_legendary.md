# Generation 3 legendaries, mythicals and mascots (Hoenn): what landed

Scope: Ruby, Sapphire, Emerald, FireRed/LeafGreen. Weather trio, Latias/Latios, Regis, Jirachi, Deoxys. We learn principles only; no creature is copied.

## Evidence (numbers)

- Pokemon of the Year 2020 (official Pokemon Co. fan vote, 6.6M+ votes): Rayquaza 8th with 60,939 votes, ahead of Gardevoir 9th (60,596). Greninja won with 140,559. Rayquaza is the only Gen 3 legendary in the top 10. Bulbapedia's table (could not be fetched directly; a search snippet quoted it, year unconfirmed) puts Kyogre 17th at 15,585 and Groudon 22nd at 11,982. So Rayquaza is roughly 4x Kyogre, though the two are the box-art mascots. (pokemondb.net; Bulbapedia via search snippet)
- Nintendo Life Gen 3 poll (Feb 2026, 2,424 votes, across the whole generation): Rayquaza 9% (most voted of the generation), Kyogre 2%, Groudon 2%, Jirachi 2%, Latias 1%, Deoxys 1%, Latios 0.9%, Regirock 0.4%, Registeel 0.2%, Regice 0%. Shares are low because the vote is split over 135 species; the ordering is the signal.
- Pokemon 2016 Japan/worldwide vote top 10 included Rayquaza (reported by Inverse).
- Pokemon Center demand signals: Latias/Latios/Pikachu plush sold with a 2-per-person limit (Fukuoka reopening, 2025); a Mega Rayquaza plush line exists at 41-45 inches.
- Critic opinion (low weight, but consistent): Sportskeeda ranks Regice last of the Hoenn legendaries; TheGamer lists Regice among bad Gen 3 designs ("an icicle with a bunch of other icicles"). Bogleech, by contrast, calls Registeel a personal favourite and Regice often a fan favourite for beauty, so the Regis polarise.

## What the designers said

- Sugimori/Masuda (Nintendo Dream, 2003, translated by Lava Cut Content): Gen 3 was meant to feel new; Sugimori unified the styles of eight designers under one art style; the team wanted "cool, monstrous" designs and drew on dinosaurs and monsters for Groudon and Kyogre, which sit on the box. Sugimori said that because of animation difficulty he chose to make Groudon cooler. Blaziken was a deliberate test of reaction to humanoids.
- Game Developer analysis: the cover legendaries are embodiments of abstract forces (ocean, earth, sky) with circuit-like markings and weather abilities, so design, mechanic and region theme all say the same thing. Latios/Latias read as modern aircraft; the Regis look artificial, matching their lore, and carry braille-like patterns.

## Patterns

1. Concept first: one huge elemental idea (sea, land, sky) readable in a single glance.
2. The sky dragon wins: Rayquaza is a long serpent with a clean silhouette, a bright colour accent (green, yellow rings) and a lore role (it calms the other two). Most-loved by a wide margin.
3. Two box-art mascots as opposing pair (red/land vs blue/sea) is strong marketing but each one individually is less loved than the unpaired third.
4. Pairing (Latias/Latios) creates affection: a sibling duo, sleek and friendly, rather than a threatening god.
5. Generic geometric/mineral designs (Regice, Registeel) rely on lore and are polarising; being abstract made them less lovable.
6. Mythicals (Jirachi, Deoxys) hold interest through scarcity and event distribution more than silhouette; Jirachi (cute, wish lore) outpolls Deoxys slightly.

## Lessons for ClayPals

- Give every rare creature one idea that fits on a sticker, repeated in colour, shape and ability.
- Make the "top rarity" a different silhouette class (long, serpentine or flying) from the common creatures so it is recognisable in a menu.
- If the box-art pair is symmetrical, give them a third, unpaired capstone with a story role.
- Rare creatures in a clay-toy style should stay chunky and friendly, not spiky and generic; cute rare ones (Latias, Jirachi) hold affection.
- Do not make rare creatures from a single repeated primitive (ice spikes, a block); add one distinctive asymmetry or face.
- Scarcity should be earned through quest or event, with a lore reason.
