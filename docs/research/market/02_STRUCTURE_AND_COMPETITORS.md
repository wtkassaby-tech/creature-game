# Structure and competitors: what the Roblox creature-RPG market teaches

Date: 16 November 2026

Status: research, nothing decided

## Summary

- The genre on Roblox is small and old. No original turn-based creature RPG holds more than about 3,500 concurrent players today; the two big "creature" games (Creatures of Sonaria, Dragon Adventures, 8,000 to 25,000 concurrent) are survival and breeding sandboxes, not story RPGs.
- The genre's defining failure is unfinished stories. Loomian Legacy launched in July 2019 and, seven years on, has shipped four of its planned eight Battle Theatres; gaps between story chapters grew from 3 months to 10, then to about 22, then to three years. Pokémon Brick Bronze was deleted (April 2018) before its ending shipped.
- Project Pokemon (wish_z, 2013) was a complete Kanto loop: 8 gyms, Elite Four, post-game exclusives, trading and PvP in a social park. It was deleted for copyright in August 2018 with about 270 million visits. Players' memories centre on finishing a full journey and on trading shinies and legendaries, not on any one mechanic.
- Brick Bronze reuploads reportedly still out-draw Loomian Legacy, which shows that demand for the classic route-town-gym structure outlasts any one game, and that original content is the only version that survives.
- Player complaints repeat across every game: long waits for story, endgame that is only rare-variant hunting at 1-in-thousands odds, rare creatures behind gamepasses or lapsed events, and steep PvP on-ramps.
- No turn-based game in the scene has visible part-based breeding. That is the clearest gap this game can own, followed by "a story that actually ends" and "a rare hunt decided by skill, not odds".
- Limits of this research: almost every source host (Rolimon's, RoWatcher, Fandom wikis, DevForum, Wikipedia, Roblox docs) was blocked for page fetches in this session, so nearly all web claims below are "snippet" confidence. Numbers should be re-checked on the live trackers before they drive a decision.

### 2.3 Lessons from the ancestors

1. The route, town, gym structure has durable demand on Roblox: people still seek out reuploads eight years later.
2. Borrowed IP is fatal, eventually and without warning. The brief's guardrail 3 (all original) is the reason this game can last. Keep creature silhouettes, capture tool, UI wording and badge language clearly its own.
3. Duplication and data resets were Project Pokemon's first wound. The brief's server authority, session locking and atomic trades address exactly this.
4. Neither ancestor's makers got to ship an ending for the audience that wanted it.

Reading (inference):

- The whole original turn-based creature RPG category is around 5,000 concurrent players on a platform that passed 12 million. A realistic early ceiling for a new entrant is hundreds to low thousands, not tens of thousands, unless it breaks the genre's mould.

## 5. First 15 minutes and discovery

What Roblox rewards (sourced, from this project's earlier research: `docs/research/FIRST_SESSION_AND_RETENTION.md` lines 46 to 48 and `docs/research/market/05_ACQUISITION_AND_LAUNCH.md` line 14, both citing Roblox docs and the June 2026 Roblox newsroom post https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/06/optimizing-discovery-great-games-reach-millions-players-roblox):

- Per-user averages, not totals: play-through rate; first-play bounce (under 60 seconds, and 61 to 180 seconds, both negative); play days per user (day 1, days 2 to 7, days 8 to 28); playtime per user capped at 60 minutes a day. Secondary: co-play days, spend days.
- The lookback window grew from 7 to 28 days in June 2026.
- Roblox's own advice is to reach the fun "ideally in 5 minutes or less".

What that means for a story RPG (inference):

- The genre's habit of opening with a long cutscene, a lab, a rival talk and a tutorial catch is exactly what the bounce signal punishes. A player must be choosing a move within the first 60 seconds and must own a second creature inside 3 minutes.
- The 28-day window and the play-days signal favour games with a reason to return on days 2 to 7 and 8 to 28. An egg timer, a daily rare spawn and a weekly beat serve this better than a long first session.
- RPGs are reported as Roblox's best-retaining genre with 45 to 90 minute sessions (snippet, vendor opinion: https://rowatcher.com/news/roblox-retention-by-genre-which-games-keep-players-longest). The 60-minute cap means sessions beyond an hour add nothing to ranking, so design for many play days, not marathon sessions.
- Co-play days count. Visible other players in hub towns, trading and casual PvP early are worth more than they look.

How the current games onboard (snippet and recall): Loomian Legacy gives a choice of seven beginners, one per type, picked in a lab in the home town; Doodle World gates the first key behind a maze. None of the turn-based games shows its endgame or its signature feature in the first session.

## 6. Recommended progression structure for the three-biome launch

All timings are starting targets to tune in playtests (inference throughout).

**First session script (target 15 minutes to "one more go")**

| Time | Beat |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00 to 0:30 | Spawn in Greenwood beside the mentor. Three starters are standing there; tap one. No cutscene before the choice |
| 0:30 to 2:00 | The rival walks up and challenges: first battle, two or three turns, cannot be lost |
| 2:00 to 4:00 | Mentor hands over a lasso. First wild creature, first lasso mini-game, guaranteed success. Player now owns two creatures |
| 4:00 to 8:00 | Route 1: a few wild battles, one local tamer, first coins, first shop visit. The giant is visible in the distance from the route |
| 8:00 to 12:00 | Mentor's breeding teaser: he breeds two of his own creatures and gifts the egg. The player sees a mixed offspring in the first session and has a timer to come back to |
| 12:00 to 15:00 | Third creature tamed, team of three complete, first quest accepted. The world map shows eleven biomes with eight in silhouette |

**Biome targets for launch**

| Biome | First clear, target | Structure | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Greenwood | 45 to 60 minutes; first badge by 60 at the latest | 1 town, 2 routes, giant, rival gate | Teach battle, lasso, giant, and a first breed |
| Dust Canyon | 75 to 90 minutes | 1 town, 2 to 3 routes, giant, gate by a local champion, rival appears as a story beat | First rarity tier, serials, breeding for Speed |
| Tide Flats | 100 to 120 minutes | 1 town, 3 routes, giant, rival gate ending on a cliffhanger | Bait, first cross-biome breeding goals |

That is about 4 to 5 hours of story at launch, matching the brief's 1.3 times curve. A Loomian chapter took its studio months to build; three biomes of this size is the right scale for one builder.

**The "interim post-game" (the part the genre always forgets)**

Launch with something to do after Tide Flats that is not waiting:

1. Breeding goals per biome: a visible checklist of part and colour combinations, with the family tree as the trophy.
2. Giant rematches at a higher tier for materials and a rarer colour chance (capture is still once per player).
3. A sealed gate to the Mire in Tide Flats town with a story reason it is shut, and a weekly "tear sighting" that shows one creature or silhouette from a later biome.
4. Weekly rotating tamer challenge with fixed rules (for example, Leaf-only 3v3).

**How to tease eight more biomes**

- The world map shows all eleven from the first minute, named but silhouetted.
- Each launch town has one traveller from a later biome with a creature the player cannot yet get.
- The level cap, the rarity table and the team-size table are shown in the UI with locked rows, so players can see what badges will open.
- The story is already written to the end (DECISIONS.md, 16 November); say so in the game. "Chapter 3 of 11" on the badge screen is a promise the competitors cannot make.

## 7. Recommended release cadence

| Beat | Frequency | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small beat | Weekly, same day each week | One of: a new creature or colour variant, a rotating challenge, a balance pass, a quest, a breeding goal set |
| Biome | Every 6 to 8 weeks; never more than 10 | One hub, routes, 8 creatures, giant, gate, badge, story chapter |
| PvP season | Every 8 to 12 weeks, offset from biome drops | Rank reset, cosmetics, labelled rewards |
| Public roadmap | Always visible in game | Next biome named with a month, not a day |

Reasoning (inference): Loomian's first two gaps (3 and 10 months) were survivable; the 22-month gap is where fan posts turn to "is it dying". Doodle World's weekly rhythm is the only growth signal in the category. At 6 to 8 weeks, eight more biomes take 12 to 16 months, which fits the brief's "2027" for milestone 11 only if each biome is a data pack, not new code. To protect the cadence:

- Have biome 4 (Mire) content-complete before soft launch, and biome 5 in progress. Ship from a buffer, not from the workbench.
- Keep launch biomes at about 8 creatures and backfill toward 15 to 25 in weekly beats. Backfill is the weekly content.
- If a biome slips, ship a "half chapter" (routes and creatures first, giant and gate two weeks later) and say so. Silence did more harm to Loomian than lateness.

## 8. Gaps in the market this game can own

1. **Visible, part-based, generational breeding in a turn-based battler.** Dragon Adventures and Creatures of Sonaria prove breeding and trading hold five-figure audiences; no turn-based story game has it. (inference from section 3.1)
2. **A story that finishes on a published schedule.** No Roblox creature RPG has delivered its full badge run since Project Pokemon, which did it with someone else's map.
3. **Rare hunting by skill and planning, not odds.** The lasso mini-game, bait crafting and breeding replace the 1-in-20,000 roll that players resent.
4. **No paywalled creatures.** The brief already sells only time, capacity and looks; competitors' most-resented monetisation is boosted rarity odds.
5. **Short-format mobile PvP.** 3v3 with low randomness and level scaling answers the "steep learning curve" complaint.
6. **A social hub from day one.** Both ancestors were loved partly as places to show off and trade. Trading is milestone 9 in the brief; the hub and creature showing-off should not wait for it.
7. **A fast start.** Fun inside 60 seconds in a genre known for slow openings.
8. **Provable scarcity.** Serials and family trees give trade value that competitors' duplicated, exploit-prone economies never had.

## Recommendations for this game

Ranked by expected effect. None changes a [Locked] item or a DECISIONS.md line unless flagged.

1. **Treat cadence as a design constraint, and build a content buffer before launch** (brief sections 13, 14). Add to the build plan: Mire content-complete before milestone 7. Keep milestone 11's "one biome per update" but attach the 6 to 8 week target and the weekly beat. Changes a [Default] (build order).
2. **Put a breeding moment in the first session** (sections 3, 7, 9). The mentor's gifted egg at about minute 10 shows the signature feature and creates a return timer. Fits the story (the mentor as breeder). Changes a [Default] only.
3. **Script the first five minutes to the discovery signals** (sections 3, 9, 12): starter choice with no prior cutscene, rival battle by 0:30, second creature by 4:00. Add first-session funnel events to the measurement list in section 12. Keeps the [Locked] "rival is one of the first battles".
4. **Ship an interim post-game with the three-biome launch** (sections 3, 8, 14): breeding checklists, harder giant rematches, a weekly challenge. Stage 2 of the roadmap ("the hardcore game") should not wait for a "dedicated following"; a small version is what creates one. Flag: this brings forward part of a [Locked] stage ordering in section 14; it does not change its content. Ask Ayrton.
5. **Show the whole journey up front** (sections 8, 9): eleven-biome map, "Chapter n of 11", in-game roadmap with months. Keep, and make visible, the DECISIONS.md line that the story is already written.
6. **Keep every creature earnable in play, permanently** (sections 6, 11). No creature locked to a lapsed event or a pass; event creatures return in a later rotation or become breedable. Keep the brief's "not for sale" list exactly as written.
7. **Make the basic lasso free or trivially cheap; sell better lassos for coins** (sections 5, 6, 10). Follows Loomian's November 2026 change and stays inside the DECISIONS.md consumables line. Changes a [Default] price only.
8. **Vary the gates and keep them short** (sections 3, 9): Greenwood and Tide Flats gated by the rival, Dust Canyon by a local champion, as the brief already proposes. Keep.
9. **Apply level caps evenly** (sections 4, 5): if a badge sets the player's level cap, NPC tamers obey the same cap. Avoids Doodle World's complaint.
10. **Bring a minimal social layer forward** (sections 8, 10, 13): shared hub towns with visible creatures following players at soft launch, and casual 3v3 as early as milestone 8 allows. Co-play days are a discovery signal. Changes a [Default] (build order).
11. **Plan for a small audience first** (sections 13, 14): the category holds about 5,000 concurrent in total. Judge the soft launch on day-1 and day-7 return and play days per user, not on concurrent players.
12. **Originality audit before the store page** (section 11, guardrail 3): no "gym", "badge case", ball-shaped capture tool, eight-badge framing or starter-trio typing that mirrors the franchise. The brief's lasso, giants and biomes already differ; keep widening that distance. Brick Bronze and Project Pokemon show what happens otherwise.
